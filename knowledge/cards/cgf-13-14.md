# CGF ch13: Longer Periods of Fasting · ch14: Extended Fasting

### CGF-13-001
claim: "You can be hungry, but you should not feel sick." Faintness, nausea or feeling unwell means stop the fast; don't push through.
why: Hunger is an expected, passing signal. Illness-like symptoms point to low blood sugar, low blood pressure or electrolyte trouble.
do: Stop, eat something gentle, and contact your doctor if symptoms don't settle quickly.
grade: D
src: CGF ch13; CGF ch14
safety: stop
when: start | long | stage:24-48 | stage:72+ | symptom:dizzy
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-13-002
claim: A 24-hour fast runs dinner to dinner: you still eat one meal that day, so family dinners and food-requiring medications stay possible.
why: Skipping breakfast and lunch gives a full day of low insulin with minimal disruption; nutrient needs are met at the one meal.
do: Two or three 24-hour fasts a week is the common starting rhythm.
grade: D
src: CGF ch13
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-13-003
claim: The 36-hour fast skips one full day (dinner on day 1 to breakfast on day 3); add a skipped breakfast and it becomes 42 hours.
why: Fung's clinic uses 36–42 h fasts three times a week for type 2 diabetes until medications are off and weight is at target, then eases off.
grade: D
src: CGF ch13
counter: That schedule ran under medical supervision with frequent glucose checks.
safety: none
when: plan
aud: t2d, advanced
tier: core

### CGF-13-004
claim: 5:2 means five normal days and two days at about 500 kcal (women) or 600 kcal (men), in one or two small meals.
why: A small allowance makes fasting days easier while still producing most of fasting's hormonal shifts; it's commonly kept as a long-term maintenance pattern.
grade: B
src: CGF ch13; Harvie 2011 Int J Obes
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-13-005
claim: Alternate-day fasting alternates normal days with ~500–600 kcal days. It works for weight loss, but more people drop out than with daily dieting.
why: Two months of ADF cut ~5.7 kg on average. In a year-long RCT it matched daily calorie restriction for weight, with higher dropout.
grade: A
src: CGF ch13; Varady 2009 Am J Clin Nutr; Trepanowski 2017 JAMA Intern Med
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-13-006
claim: After a longer fast, eat normally to fullness with whole, low-sugar food. Don't add calorie restriction on top.
why: The fast already creates the deficit. Stacking deliberate calorie cuts afterward is hard to sustain and invites metabolic slowing and rebound.
grade: D
src: CGF ch13
safety: none
when: break | eat
aud: all

### CGF-13-007
claim: Waist size tells you more about metabolic health than weight. Aim to keep your waist below half your height.
why: Waist circumference reflects visceral fat around the organs, the fat most tied to diabetes and heart disease.
do: Measure at the navel once a month; log it next to your fasts.
grade: A
src: CGF ch13; NICE 2022 obesity guideline (waist-to-height ratio)
safety: none

### CGF-14-001
claim: In a 1968 hospital study, 44 of 46 people completed a 14-day fast on calorie-free drinks, with almost no hunger after day one.
why: Patients reported well-being, even euphoria. Average loss was ~7.8 kg; all three insulin-treated diabetics came off insulin. Half didn't keep to the follow-up diet at home.
grade: B
src: CGF ch14; Gilliland 1968 Postgrad Med J
safety: none
when: history | long
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-14-002
claim: The longest recorded fast lasted 382 days under medical supervision: weight fell from 207 to 82 kg, and was 89 kg five years later.
why: With non-caloric fluids, vitamins and monitored supplements, his body ran on its own fat while blood sugar stayed normal.
grade: B
src: CGF ch14; Stewart & Fleming 1973 Postgrad Med J
counter: An extreme, closely supervised case, not a template. Fung says blood sugar stayed "well within normal"; the paper reports ~30 mg/dL for the last 8 months (asymptomatic), plus low magnesium and temporary high calcium (PMID 4803438).
safety: none
when: history
aud: all
tier: tail

### SCI-14-001
claim: The "metabolic switch" from burning mostly glucose to mostly fat and ketones typically happens 12–36 hours after the last meal.
why: Timing depends on how full liver glycogen was. A carb-heavy dinner and inactivity push it later; low-carb eating and exercise bring it sooner.
do: A walk or workout during the fast, and a lower-carb last meal, both speed up the switch.
grade: B
src: Anton 2018 Obesity; Cahill 2006 Annu Rev Nutr
counter: Fung writes that ketones take 36–48 h to "ramp up"; that's when they become the brain's major fuel, but blood ketones start rising much earlier.
safety: none
when: stage:12-20 | stage:20-24 | start
aud: all
tier: core
note: STAGE AUDIT: supports app's 12–18 h fat-burning and 18–24 h ketosis stages as "typical", if worded as variable.

### CGF-14-003
claim: Expect about 0.25 kg (½ lb) of fat lost per fasting day; 45 kg of excess fat equals roughly 200 days of complete fasting.
why: Studies of long fasts show 0.2–0.35 kg/day once water is excluded, matching a steady ~2,000 kcal/day burn with no metabolic shutdown.

### CGF-14-004
claim: Blood electrolytes stay normal in most extended fasts. Magnesium is the one that more often runs low, especially in diabetes.
why: Most magnesium sits inside cells, so blood tests underestimate stores; Fung's clinic routinely supplements it during long fasts.
do: For fasts beyond a few days, ask your doctor about magnesium; muscle cramps are a hint it may be low.
grade: B
src: CGF ch14; Stewart & Fleming 1973 Postgrad Med J
safety: none
when: long | symptom:cramps
aud: all, t2d
tier: core

### CGF-14-005
claim: Few bowel movements during a long fast are normal and usually painless. The 382-day faster went over a month between them.
why: With no food coming in, little stool forms; discomfort comes from a full colon, which a fasting colon isn't.
do: No action needed unless uncomfortable. Eat more fiber and vegetables on eating days; psyllium helps; ask your doctor if it persists.
grade: B
src: CGF ch14; CGF ch15; CGF intro
safety: none
when: symptom:constipation | long
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-14-006
claim: Fung's clinic rarely ends fasts at 2–3 days. Day 2 is hardest, so for supervised patients it continues to 7–14 days.
why: After day 2, hunger drops and benefits continue to build. Stopping right after the hardest point wastes the easier stretch that follows.
grade: D
src: CGF ch14
counter: Clinic practice with monitoring. Unsupervised fasts beyond ~3 days carry real risks (SCI-02-001, CGF-14-007).
safety: none
when: long | plan
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-14-007
claim: Refeeding syndrome is a rare but dangerous shift of phosphorus, magnesium and potassium when eating resumes after a long fast.
why: Food raises insulin, which pulls minerals into cells to rebuild glycogen and protein. Blood levels can crash, causing weakness, confusion, cramps or heart-rhythm problems.
do: After fasts of 5+ days, restart with small meals, and seek care for weakness, palpitations, confusion or breathlessness.
grade: A
src: CGF ch14; Crook 2001 Nutrition; NICE CG32
safety: stop
when: break | long | stage:72+
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-14-008
claim: Highest refeeding risk: BMI under 18.5, over 10% weight lost in 6 months, alcohol misuse, eating disorder, cancer, or bowel disease.
why: These states deplete mineral stores already, so a fast followed by refeeding can tip them into dangerous deficiency.
do: If any of these apply, don't do extended fasts; at most short fasts under 24 h, with your doctor's agreement.
grade: A
src: CGF ch14; NICE CG32
safety: stop

### CGF-14-010
claim: Keep moving during extended fasts. Normal activity and exercise help preserve muscle and bone.
why: Disuse, not fasting itself, drove much of the muscle and bone loss in David Blaine's 44-day fast in a box, where he couldn't stand or walk.
do: Walk daily and keep light strength work going; skip intense sessions if you feel faint.
grade: B
src: CGF ch14
safety: none

### CGF-14-011
claim: Ankle and foot swelling after a long fast is a known, usually temporary effect of refeeding.
why: Rising insulin makes the kidneys retain salt and water again ("refeeding edema"). It normally settles within days.
do: Keep salt moderate in the first days of eating. See a doctor if swelling is severe or comes with breathlessness.
grade: B
src: CGF ch14
safety: caution

# SCI iteration 3: psychology and neuroscience of fasting behaviour (PubMed-verified where cited)

### SCI-92-001
claim: After breaking a diet rule, people often abandon restraint entirely and eat far more: the "what-the-hell" effect.
why: Rigid all-or-nothing rules turn one slip into a perceived total failure. Restrained eaters overeat more after a "forbidden" preload than unrestrained eaters.
do: A slip is one meal, not a broken day. Resume at the next planned point.
grade: B
src: Polivy & Herman 1985 Am Psychol (PMID 3857016)
safety: none
when: break | between | hunger
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-002
claim: Being kind to yourself after a lapse reduces later overeating; self-criticism increases it.
why: Guilt and shame are aversive states people escape by eating. Self-compassion lowers the distress without excusing the behavior.
grade: B
src: Adams & Leary 2007 J Soc Clin Psychol
counter: Single laboratory study in restrictive eaters; replications are mixed but consistent in direction.
safety: none
when: break | between
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-003
claim: "Urge surfing" (noticing a craving's body sensations and letting it crest and pass without acting) weakened the link between craving and acting on it.
why: A craving is a time-limited wave; observing it non-judgmentally breaks the automatic craving-to-action chain. Shown in a mindfulness trial with smokers.
do: When a craving hits, name where you feel it, breathe slowly, and watch it peak and fade.
grade: B
src: Bowen & Marlatt 2009 Psychol Addict Behav (PMID 20025372)
counter: Tested for smoking, not food; mechanisms are shared, food-specific evidence is smaller.
safety: none
when: hunger
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-004
claim: Thinking about a food's long-term consequences reduces how much you crave it and engages the brain's prefrontal control regions.
why: Cognitive reappraisal ("later" framing) recruits prefrontal cortex and dampens reward-region activity to the craved cue.
do: Before an unplanned bite, picture how you'll feel in two hours, and tomorrow.
grade: B
src: Kober 2010 Proc Natl Acad Sci USA (PMID 20679212)
safety: none
when: hunger | eat
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-005
claim: How strongly you react to food cues (sight, smell, ads) predicts how much you eat and later weight gain.
why: Cue-induced craving is a measurable, robust driver of eating across dozens of studies, so reducing exposure is a legitimate strategy, not weakness.
grade: A
src: Boswell & Kober 2016 Obes Rev (PMID 26644270)
safety: none
when: hunger | plan
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-006
claim: Craving ("wanting") and enjoyment ("liking") run on different brain systems. The urge can far exceed the pleasure the food actually gives.
why: Dopamine drives anticipation and pursuit; separate opioid "hotspots" generate pleasure. Cues can inflate wanting without increasing liking.
grade: B
src: Berridge 2009 Physiol Behav (PMID 19336238)
safety: none
when: hunger
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-007
claim: People eat substantially more when eating with others than alone; the effect is strongest among friends and family.
why: Shared meals lengthen eating and set social norms for portions. A meta-analysis found reliable social facilitation of intake.
do: Plan fasts around social meals rather than fighting them; decide your portion before you sit down.
grade: A
src: Ruddock 2019 Am J Clin Nutr (PMID 31435639)
safety: none
when: plan | eat | between
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-008
claim: New weeks, months, birthdays and holidays boost motivation to start fresh: the "fresh start" effect.
why: Temporal landmarks create a mental break from past failures, making a new beginning feel credible.
do: Use a landmark to restart, but don't wait for one: tomorrow is a landmark too.
grade: B
src: Dai & Li 2019 Curr Opin Psychol (PMID 29753243)
safety: none
when: between | plan
aud: all
tier: tail

### SCI-92-009
claim: Effort rises as a goal gets closer (the goal-gradient effect), so near milestones keep you going better than one distant finish line.
why: Perceived progress toward a near target increases motivation; long goals feel abstract until broken into visible steps.
do: Aim for the next milestone on the dial, not the end of the fast.
grade: B
src: Kivetz 2006 J Mark Res
safety: none
when: start | stage:12-20 | stage:24-48 | long
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-010
claim: One night of poor sleep makes junk food more desirable: brain control regions quiet down while reward regions light up.
why: Sleep deprivation reduces frontal-cortex activity in appetite evaluation and amplifies amygdala responses to high-calorie foods.
grade: B
src: Greer 2013 Nat Commun (PMID 23922121)
safety: none
when: hunger | symptom:insomnia | between
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-011
claim: Much "hunger" is boredom. People eat to escape the empty feeling of being bored, especially when the food is a distraction.
why: Eating gives quick stimulation and shifts attention away from an aversive state of low engagement.
do: Ask "am I hungry or under-stimulated?" A task, a walk or a conversation often answers it.
grade: B
src: Moynihan 2015 Front Psychol (PMID 25883579)
safety: none
when: hunger
aud: all
tier: core

### SCI-92-012
claim: Many people report better mood, calm and alertness after the first days of a fast, and early clinical studies point the same way.
why: Proposed mechanisms include ketones as brain fuel, rising BDNF and noradrenaline, and changes in serotonin signaling.
grade: C
src: Fond 2013 Psychiatry Res (PMID 23332541)
counter: Mostly small, uncontrolled studies; expectation effects are large. People with mood disorders should fast only with their clinician.
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | stage:54-72
aud: all
tier: tail

### SCI-92-013
claim: Every fast you complete builds confidence that you can do the next one. Mastery experience is the strongest source of self-efficacy.
why: Self-efficacy grows most from personal success, not persuasion; it predicts whether people keep a new behavior.
do: Start with fasts you can finish, then stretch. Counting completed fasts is more useful than counting failures.
grade: B
src: Bandura 1977 Psychol Rev
safety: none
when: break | between | start
aud: all
tier: core

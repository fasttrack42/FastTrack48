# CGF ch7: Fasting for a Younger, Smarter You · ch8: Fasting for Heart Health

### CGF-07-001
claim: Short fasts don't dull your mind. Controlled studies found no loss of attention, reaction time, memory or mood over up to two days.
why: The brain is protected first during food scarcity; ketones and noradrenaline keep it fueled and alert.
do: You can work, study and drive normally on fasting days. If you feel foggy, check salt and fluids first.
grade: B
src: CGF ch7; Green 1995 J Psychiatr Res; Lieberman 2008 Am J Clin Nutr
counter: Some studies find mild irritability or slower performance on specific tasks early in fasting, especially in first-time fasters.
safety: none
when: stage:12-20 | stage:20-24 | myth
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-07-003
claim: Post-feast sleepiness comes from the sheer size of the meal, not from tryptophan in the turkey.
why: Turkey has no more tryptophan than other poultry. Large meals trigger hormonal and nervous-system shifts that favor rest and digestion.
grade: B
src: CGF ch7
counter: Fung blames blood being diverted from the brain; brain blood flow is tightly regulated, so hormonal and vagal effects are the better explanation.

### CGF-07-004
claim: In animals, intermittent fasting improves learning and memory and slows brain aging, partly by raising BDNF, a nerve-growth factor.
why: Both fasting and exercise raise BDNF in rodent brains. Fasted mice show fewer signs of Alzheimer's-, Parkinson's- and Huntington's-like disease.
grade: C
src: CGF ch7; Mattson 2012 Cell Metab; Longo & Mattson 2014 Cell Metab
counter: Animal data. Human trials on fasting and long-term cognition are small and inconclusive.
safety: none
when: stage:24-48
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-07-006
claim: Autophagy is the cell's recycling system: it breaks down worn-out proteins and parts and reuses the material.
why: When nutrients are scarce, cells dismantle their oldest components for fuel and building blocks, clearing damage that otherwise builds up with age.
grade: A
src: CGF ch7; Mizushima 2007 Genes Dev; Ohsumi Nobel 2016
safety: none
when: stage:24-48
aud: all

### CGF-07-007
claim: Eating, especially protein and carbohydrate, switches autophagy off through the mTOR nutrient sensor; constant snacking keeps it suppressed.
why: Insulin and amino acids activate mTOR, which tells cells food is plentiful and blocks recycling. Fat stores don't count; only incoming nutrients do.
do: Long gaps between meals, with no snacking, give cells time with mTOR switched off.
grade: B
src: CGF ch7; Glick 2010 J Pathol
counter: Fung's "3 g of leucine stops autophagy" cites a muscle-growth study (Glynn 2010), not one measuring autophagy. The direction is right; the threshold is unproven.
safety: none
when: between | stage:24-48
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-07-008
claim: Fasting increases autophagy in humans, but nobody knows when it starts or peaks. Popular "16 h" or "24 h" thresholds come from animal studies.
why: Human autophagy can't yet be measured directly in living tissue. Markers rise in muscle and blood after 24–36 h fasts and with time-restricted eating.
grade: C
src: CGF ch7; Vendelbo 2014 PLoS One; Jamshed 2019 Nutrients
counter: Fung presents fasting as the strongest autophagy trigger. Plausible, but human evidence is indirect, and calorie restriction also induces autophagy in animals.
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | stage:20-24 | myth
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-07-009
claim: In Alzheimer's, the brain's autophagy system fails to clear amyloid protein clumps; boosting autophagy is a research target.
why: Brain cells normally package amyloid-beta for disposal through autophagy. When the pathway stalls, amyloid accumulates inside neurons.
grade: C
src: CGF ch7; Nilsson 2013 Cell Rep; Cataldo 2000 Am J Pathol
counter: That fasting prevents Alzheimer's in people is unproven.
safety: none
when: stage:24-48
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-07-010
claim: Fasting is not a cancer treatment. With cancer, fast only under your oncologist; the relationship between autophagy and cancer cuts both ways.
why: Autophagy can prevent cancer from starting but can also help established tumors survive stress. Weight loss can be dangerous during cancer therapy.
do: If you have cancer or are in treatment, don't fast without your oncology team. Some trials study fasting-mimicking diets alongside chemo.
grade: B
src: CGF ch7; Hippert 2006 Cancer Res; de Groot 2020 Nat Commun
counter: Fung cites Seyfried's annual 7-day fast for cancer prevention; that is a hypothesis without human outcome data.
safety: caution
when: onboarding | long
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-07-011
claim: Multi-day fasts in mice clear old immune cells and trigger regeneration from stem cells when refeeding begins.
why: Prolonged fasting lowers IGF-1 and PKA signaling, pushing blood stem cells to self-renew. Refeeding rebuilds a younger immune population.
grade: C
src: CGF ch7; Cheng 2014 Cell Stem Cell
counter: Mostly mouse data; human evidence is a small pilot in chemotherapy patients. "Immune reset" in healthy people is unproven.
safety: none
when: stage:72+
aud: all
tier: core
note: STAGE AUDIT: app's 72 h+ stage presents this as human fact; reword to "in animal studies" and keep the Cheng citation.

### CGF-08-001
claim: Triglycerides, a blood fat made by the liver from excess carbohydrate, predict heart risk almost as strongly as LDL.
why: When glycogen is full, the liver turns surplus sugar into triglycerides, exported as VLDL, the precursor of LDL. Cutting refined carbs and fasting lower them quickly.
grade: A
src: CGF ch8; Miller 2011 Circulation
safety: none
when: eat | between
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-08-002
claim: For most people, cholesterol in food (eggs, shellfish) has only a small effect on blood cholesterol; the liver makes most of it.
why: The liver produces roughly 80% of blood cholesterol and makes less when more is eaten.
grade: B
src: CGF ch8; Keys 1953 J Mt Sinai Hosp
counter: About a quarter of people are "hyper-responders". Saturated fat (not dietary cholesterol) does raise LDL in controlled feeding trials (Mensink 2016 WHO).
safety: none

aud: all

### CGF-08-003
claim: Fung argues dietary fat doesn't raise blood cholesterol and that low-fat guidelines were never supported by trials.
why: He cites Framingham's unpublished diet data, Tecumseh and Harcombe's 2015 meta-analysis of the trials available in 1977–83.
grade: D
src: CGF ch8; Harcombe 2015 Open Heart; Nichols 1976 Am J Clin Nutr
counter: Saturated fat raises LDL in controlled feeding studies, and replacing it with unsaturated fat lowers heart events. Contested; not for in-app claims.

### CGF-08-004
claim: In a 10-week alternate-day fasting trial, LDL fell ~25% and triglycerides ~30%, while protective HDL held steady.
why: Less incoming carbohydrate means the liver makes fewer triglycerides and less VLDL, the source of LDL. Fat loss improves the whole lipid profile.
grade: B
src: CGF ch8; Bhutani 2010 Obesity
counter: Small trial in obese adults, largely mediated by weight loss. Lean people can see LDL rise during prolonged fasts (CGF-00-019).
safety: none
when: between | onboarding
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-08-005
claim: Fasting can improve blood pressure, sugar and lipids, but it's not a reason to stop prescribed heart or cholesterol medication on your own.
why: Medications protect against events that fasting's improvements may not fully cover yet. Doses should be reduced deliberately, with monitoring.
do: Share your fasting plan with your doctor and let them taper statins or blood-pressure drugs if your numbers improve.
grade: A
src: CGF ch8; CGF ch6
counter: Fung highlights statin side effects (diabetes risk is real but small; the Alzheimer's link isn't supported).
safety: caution
when: onboarding | plan
aud: meds
tier: core

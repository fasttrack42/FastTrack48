# CGF ch1: What Is Fasting?

### CGF-01-001
claim: Fasting is voluntary and can end any moment you choose; starvation is forced, with no control and no end in sight.
why: The physiology overlaps, but control changes everything: you start when ready, carry stored fuel, and stop at will.
grade: D
src: CGF ch1
safety: none
when: myth | onboarding
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-01-002
claim: Everyone already fasts daily. "Breakfast" literally means the meal that breaks the overnight fast.
why: Any time you aren't eating, you're fasting; deliberate fasting just extends a normal daily state.
grade: D
src: CGF ch1
safety: none
when: onboarding | history
aud: beginner
tier: tail

### CGF-01-003
claim: Adults now eat about 5–6 times a day, up from about 3 in the late 1970s. The daily fasting window has nearly vanished.
why: With 3 meals, ~14 h of each day was fasted. Constant snacking keeps insulin up most waking hours, leaving little time to burn stores.
do: Cutting snacks is the first, easiest fast. Three meals with nothing between already restores a daily low-insulin window.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1; Popkin & Duffey 2010 Am J Clin Nutr; Gill & Panda 2015 Cell Metab
counter: Eating frequency rose alongside obesity; that it causes obesity is not proven.
safety: none
when: between | onboarding
aud: all
tier: core
note: Gill & Panda: more than half of adults spread eating over 15 h or more per day.

### CGF-01-004
claim: Protein raises insulin too. Some proteins (whey, beef) raise it about as much as some starchy foods; fat raises it least.
why: Amino acids stimulate insulin release directly. Fat barely does, but fat is rarely eaten alone.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1; Holt 1997 Am J Clin Nutr
counter: Protein also raises glucagon, which offsets some effects; protein is highly satiating and needed for muscle.
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-01-005
claim: The body has two modes: fed (insulin high, storing energy) and fasted (insulin low, burning stores). Weight follows the balance of time between them.
why: Insulin is the switch. Long fed periods favor storage; regular fasted periods let stored glycogen and fat be spent.
do: Think in time, not just calories: aim for real stretches each day where insulin can fall.
grade: D
src: CGF ch1
counter: A useful model; energy balance still applies, and calories within the eating window still matter.
safety: none
when: onboarding | stage:12-20
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-01-006
claim: Liver glycogen covers roughly a day of glucose needs. As it runs down, the liver makes new glucose and fat burning takes over.
why: The liver holds ~100 g glycogen. Gluconeogenesis rises within hours and supplies most glucose by ~22 h, fueled partly by glycerol from fat.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1; Cahill 2006 Annu Rev Nutr; Rothman 1991 Science
counter: Fung says glycogen lasts 24–36 h; Rothman shows gluconeogenesis already ~64% of output in the first 22 h. Timing varies with prior meals and activity.
safety: none
when: stage:8-12 | stage:12-20 | stage:20-24
aud: all
tier: core
note: STAGE AUDIT: app's 8–12 h "gluconeogenesis" and 12–18 h "fat burning" fit Rothman better than Fung's 24 h–2 d. Keep app timing; cite Rothman.

### CGF-01-007
claim: After a few days of fasting, ketones supply a large share of the brain's energy, sparing glucose and therefore muscle protein.
why: Fat can't cross into the brain, but liver-made ketones can. Blood β-hydroxybutyrate can rise over 70-fold, cutting the brain's need for glucose.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1; Owen 1967 J Clin Invest; Cahill 2006 Annu Rev Nutr
counter: Fung says ~75% at 4 days. Owen measured ~60–70% after 5–6 weeks; at day 3–4 the share is lower, closer to a third or half.
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | stage:54-72 | long
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-01-008
claim: Protein breakdown is highest in the first days of fasting, then drops sharply as ketones take over. It falls; it never stops.
why: Early glucose comes partly from amino acids. As ketones rise, nitrogen loss falls to a fraction, sparing muscle.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1; Cahill 2006 Annu Rev Nutr
counter: Fung says that from day 5 protein "is not being used for energy". It is used, at a low rate (about 20–30 g protein/day).
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | stage:72+ | myth
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-01-009
claim: Lowering insulin makes the kidneys shed salt and water. Early weight drop, less bloating and lower blood pressure are common.
why: Insulin signals the kidneys to retain sodium. When it falls, sodium and water leave, which also lowers blood pressure.
do: If you take blood-pressure pills or diuretics, ask your doctor first. Fasting can push pressure too low.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1
safety: caution
when: start | stage:24-48 | onboarding
aud: all, meds
tier: core

### CGF-01-010
claim: Blood electrolytes usually stay in normal range even in very long supervised fasts; the body guards them tightly.
why: Bones hold 99% of calcium, magnesium and phosphorus, and the kidneys cut losses. Potassium may dip slightly but stays normal.
do: For fasts longer than a few days, a multivitamin and salt cover the gaps; supervised fasts check blood levels.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1; Stewart & Fleming 1973 Postgrad Med J; Drenick 1964 JAMA
counter: Normal blood levels don't prove normal body stores. In the 382-day fast, potassium needed correcting early and plasma magnesium stayed low throughout (Stewart & Fleming 1973, PMID 4803438). The real danger comes at refeeding (ch14).
safety: caution
when: long | fluids
aud: all
tier: core
note: VERIFIED Stewart & Fleming abstract. Drenick 1964 complications still unchecked.

### CGF-01-011
claim: Children, pregnant and breastfeeding women should not fast for health; their mineral and energy needs are ongoing.
why: Growth, pregnancy and milk production need a continuous supply of nutrients that body stores shouldn't be drawn down to cover.
do: If you're pregnant, breastfeeding or under 18, don't use extended fasting.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1; CGF ch10
safety: stop
when: onboarding | plan
aud: women, all
tier: core

### CGF-01-012
claim: Metabolism tends to rise slightly in short fasts. Resting energy use was up ~12% after four days without food.
why: Noradrenaline rises to release stored fuel and keep you alert, so you have the energy to find food rather than shutting down.
do: Expect alertness, not sluggishness, on days 2–4. Fatigue usually signals low salt, not a slowing metabolism.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1; Zauner 2000 Am J Clin Nutr; YT tIuj-oMN-Fk
counter: Small study (n=11) of lean adults. Much longer fasts do eventually reduce energy expenditure.
safety: none
when: stage:54-72 | myth
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-01-013
claim: Fasting is the strongest natural booster of growth hormone: about 5-fold over a 2-day fast.
why: Meals and high insulin suppress GH. Without food its pulses get bigger and more frequent, which helps release fat and limit muscle-protein breakdown.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1; Hartman 1992 J Clin Endocrinol Metab; Ho 1988 J Clin Invest; Nørrelund 2001 Diabetes
counter: Fasting GH is a fuel-sparing signal, not an anti-aging or anabolic one. IGF-1 falls at the same time. Small studies in lean men.
safety: none
when: stage:48-54
aud: all
tier: core
note: STAGE AUDIT: app attributes "5-fold over ~2 days" to Ho 1988. Ho 1988 = 5-day fast (~2x+ integrated GH). The 2-day 5-fold figure is Hartman 1992. Fix the citation.

### CGF-01-014
claim: A large meal can suppress growth hormone by as much as 80%.
why: Overeating raises insulin sharply, and high insulin is a strong brake on growth-hormone release.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1; Cornford 2011 J Clin Endocrinol Metab
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-01-015
claim: The body's hormones peak around 4 a.m., releasing sugar before you wake. Morning blood sugar can rise without food.
why: Growth hormone, cortisol and adrenaline counter insulin to prepare fuel for the day: the "dawn phenomenon".
do: A higher morning reading while fasting is normal and not a sign the fast is failing.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1; CGF ch6
safety: none
when: stage:12-20 | stage:24-48
aud: t2d, all
tier: core

### CGF-01-016
claim: Ultra-processed food makes people eat more. In a controlled trial, it added ~500 kcal/day compared with whole foods matched for nutrients.
why: Processing strips fiber and structure, speeds eating and absorption, and weakens fullness signals, so the same "macros" behave very differently.
do: Break fasts and fill eating windows with recognizable whole foods; avoid packaged products.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1; Hall 2019 Cell Metab
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-01-017
claim: Sugar and refined flour are the foods most worth cutting; they raise insulin most and fill you least.
why: Finely milled, fiber-stripped carbohydrates absorb fast, spike glucose and insulin, and leave hunger soon after.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1
counter: Low-carb and low-fat diets come out similar for weight in long RCTs (DIETFITS, Gardner 2018 JAMA). Food quality matters more than macro ratios, which is Fung's point too.
safety: none
when: eat
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-01-018
claim: Industrial trans fats harm the heart; natural fats in olive oil, nuts, avocado and fish are protective.
why: Trans fats raise LDL and lower HDL. Mediterranean-style fats reduced cardiovascular events in a large RCT.
grade: A
src: CGF ch1; Estruch 2018 N Engl J Med (PREDIMED)
safety: none
when: eat
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-01-019
claim: Fung considers seed oils (corn, sunflower, canola) inflammatory because of their omega-6 content.
why: His view: modern diets carry far more omega-6 than omega-3, tipping the balance toward inflammation.
grade: D
src: CGF ch1
counter: Controlled trials find dietary linoleic acid does not raise inflammatory markers, and it's linked to lower heart risk. Contested; not for in-app claims.
safety: none
when: eat
aud: advanced
tier: drop
note: Johnson & Fritsche 2012 J Acad Nutr Diet; Farvid 2014 Circulation. Keep for audit only.

### CGF-01-020
claim: Dry fasting (no food and no water) is harder and riskier, and adds no health benefit over a water fast.
why: Dehydration adds strain on kidneys and blood pressure, limits safe duration, and raises complication risk without improving the metabolic effects.
do: On religious dry-fast days keep them short; health fasts should always include water.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1
safety: caution
when: fastday | fluids | plan
aud: all
tier: core
note: Links to the lunar/Ekadashi feature (nirjala fasts are dry).

### CGF-01-021
claim: While fasting, water and plain or herbal tea are all fine.
why: They carry little or no energy, so insulin stays low, while easing hunger and supplying fluid and salt.
grade: D
src: CGF ch1; CGF ch11
safety: none
when: fluids | start
aud: all

### CGF-01-022
claim: PCOS is closely tied to insulin resistance, and lowering insulin can restore cycles and reduce symptoms.
why: High insulin drives the ovaries to overproduce androgens. Reducing it is a first-line approach, and weight loss often restores ovulation.
grade: B
src: CGF ch1 (Samantha)
counter: Fasting-specific PCOS trials are small; the link to insulin resistance is well established.
safety: none
when: onboarding
aud: women
tier: tail

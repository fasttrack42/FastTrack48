# CGF ch5: Fasting for Weight Loss

### CGF-05-001
claim: Weight regain after dieting is biology, not weak willpower. Nearly every calorie-cutting diet plateaus and rebounds.
why: In a UK cohort of ~175,000 obese adults, under 1% reached normal weight per year. In a huge 7-year trial, women cutting ~360 kcal/day ended up less than 1 kg lighter.
do: If past diets failed, it wasn't a character flaw. Change the strategy, not your self-judgment.
grade: A
src: CGF ch5; Fildes 2015 Am J Public Health; Howard 2006 JAMA
safety: none
when: onboarding | plateau
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-05-002
claim: Six years after The Biggest Loser, most contestants had regained the weight, and they still burned ~700 kcal/day less than expected.
why: Large, sustained calorie deficits provoke lasting metabolic adaptation: the body defends its former weight by spending less energy.
grade: A
src: CGF ch5; Fothergill 2016 Obesity; Johannsen 2012 J Clin Endocrinol Metab; YT tIuj-oMN-Fk
counter: n=14. Adaptation tracked the degree of energy deficit and leptin rather than insulin (Knuth 2014 Obesity).
safety: none
when: myth | plateau
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-05-003
claim: Glycogen is a fridge; body fat is a basement freezer. You mostly use the fridge first, and insulin decides whether the freezer door is open.
why: Glycogen is small and quick to access; fat is vast and slower. High insulin blocks fat release; low insulin unlocks it, even before glycogen is fully empty.
do: Every hour after insulin falls and the "fridge" empties is an hour you're drawing on the freezer. That's what fasting buys.
grade: B
src: CGF ch5; YT tIuj-oMN-Fk
counter: Insulin's block on fat release is textbook (A). Fung's broader claim that insulin, not energy balance, is the root cause of obesity is contested (Hall 2015 Cell Metab).
safety: none
when: stage:12-20 | onboarding
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-05-004
claim: Insulin is the brake on fat burning. Even modest insulin levels sharply suppress the release of fat from fat cells.
why: Insulin inhibits hormone-sensitive lipase. Fat cells release fatty acids freely only when insulin is low.
grade: A
src: CGF ch5
safety: none
when: stage:12-20 | eat
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-05-005
claim: Before insulin was discovered, children with type 1 diabetes wasted away however much they ate. It shows insulin's power over fat storage.
why: With no insulin, fat is released constantly and can't be stored. Insulin injections restored normal weight within weeks (1922).
grade: A
src: CGF ch5
safety: none
when: history
aud: all
tier: tail

### SCI-05-001
claim: After weight loss, appetite rises by roughly 100 kcal/day for every kilogram lost: about three times more than metabolism slows.
why: The body defends its prior weight mostly through hunger. That's why maintenance feels harder than the loss itself.
do: Expect stronger hunger after losing weight. Regular fasting and protein-rich, unprocessed meals help hold the line.
grade: B
src: Polidori 2016 Obesity (PMID 27804272)
safety: none
when: plateau | hunger | between
aud: all
tier: core
note: VERIFIED citation; derived from SGLT2-inhibitor trial data modelling energy intake.

### CGF-05-006
claim: In type 2 diabetes, a carb-free diet delivered about 71% of the glucose-lowering benefit of a fast, at equal calories.
why: Removing carbohydrate removes most of the insulin stimulus. Fasting also removes protein's insulin effect, cutting insulin further.
do: If low-carb alone has stalled your blood sugar, intermittent fasting is the next lever (with your doctor if on medication).
grade: B
src: CGF ch5; Nuttall 2015 Metabolism
counter: Short study, small n. Shows glucose effects, not long-term outcomes.
safety: none
when: plan | plateau
aud: t2d
tier: tail

### CGF-05-007
claim: Two low-calorie days a week (5:2) gave the same weight loss as daily calorie cutting but a larger improvement in insulin sensitivity.
why: Total weekly calories were nearly equal; the intermittent pattern produced deeper, repeated low-insulin periods.
grade: B
src: CGF ch5; Harvie 2011 Int J Obes
counter: n=107 women, 6 months. Later trials show mostly similar metabolic effects at equal weight loss. The insulin advantage is modest and not always replicated.
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-05-008
claim: Restricting some foods all of the time is a different strategy from restricting all foods some of the time.
why: Constant restriction invites constant adaptation. Intermittent restriction alternates states, which may resist the body's adjustment.
grade: D
src: CGF ch5
counter: A useful framing; head-to-head trials show similar weight loss. The edge is in adherence and insulin, not magic.
safety: none
when: onboarding | plan
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-05-009
claim: Surgically removing 10 kg of fat by liposuction gave no improvement in blood sugar, insulin action or heart-risk markers.
why: Metabolic harm comes from fat in the liver, muscle and around organs, and from high insulin, not from the amount of fat under the skin.
grade: B
src: CGF ch5; Klein 2004 N Engl J Med
safety: none
when: myth
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-05-010
claim: Diabetes often improves within days after bariatric surgery, and an equally restrictive diet without surgery does as well or better.
why: The early benefit comes largely from the sudden, severe drop in food intake: in effect, enforced fasting.
grade: B
src: CGF ch5; Lingvay 2013 Diabetes Care
counter: Long-term weight outcomes still favor surgery for severe obesity. Fasting isn't proven equivalent over years.
safety: none
when: myth
aud: t2d, advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-05-011
claim: Ketosis from fasting is normal and safe; diabetic ketoacidosis is a different, dangerous state that needs a severe lack of insulin.
why: In ketosis, insulin is low but present and ketones are burned as fuel. In ketoacidosis, almost no insulin lets ketones and glucose pile up to toxic levels.
do: Type 1 diabetes, or SGLT2-inhibitor drugs (names ending in "-gliflozin"): don't fast without your doctor, since ketoacidosis can occur even with normal sugar.
grade: A
src: CGF ch5
counter: Fung says non-diabetics can't develop ketoacidosis. Rare exceptions exist, e.g. lactation ketoacidosis in breastfeeding women.
safety: stop
when: onboarding | stage:20-24 | myth
aud: all, meds
tier: core

### CGF-05-012
claim: Short and intermittent fasts generally don't raise cortisol, the stress hormone. Most people feel no stress-hormone effect.
why: Two weeks of intermittent fasting left cortisol unchanged, and 72-hour fasts show little change.
do: If fasting leaves you wired, anxious or sleepless, shorten fasts or change approach.
grade: B
src: CGF ch5; Soeters 2009 Am J Clin Nutr
counter: Five-day fasts can amplify cortisol release (Bergendahl 1996 J Clin Endocrinol Metab). Individual responses vary; Fung notes some patients.
safety: none
when: symptom:insomnia | myth | long
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-05-013
claim: Real fat loss while fasting is about 0.25 kg (½ lb) per day. Anything faster is water, which returns when you eat.
why: A full day's energy need (~2,000 kcal) divided by ~7,700 kcal per kg of fat comes to about a quarter-kilo. The rest of a 1 kg drop is glycogen and its water.
do: Read the scale through this lens: water returns on eating, but the quarter-kilo of fat per fasted day is real progress.
grade: A
src: CGF ch5
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | long | break | plateau
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-05-014
claim: Plateaus are expected: weight lost on fasting days eventually balances weight regained on eating days.
why: Any steady routine reaches equilibrium. Changing the stimulus restarts progress.
do: Change the pattern: 24 h → 36 h → 48 h, one meal a day, or an occasional longer fast. Also check snacking and food quality.
grade: D
src: CGF ch5
safety: none
when: plateau
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-05-015
claim: The longer you've been overweight, and if you take insulin, the slower fasting results tend to come. Persistence matters more than speed.
why: Long-standing insulin resistance and injected insulin keep fat stores harder to access, so early progress can be slow.
grade: D
src: CGF ch5
safety: none
when: plateau | onboarding
aud: all, t2d
tier: tail

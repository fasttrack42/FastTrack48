### YT-03-001
claim: Fung argues the case for low-salt diets is weaker than taught: average blood-pressure drops are small and partly inflated by publication bias.
why: He cites a 34-trial meta-analysis (−5.4/−2.4 mmHg), funnel-plot asymmetry, a 2015 Intersalt reanalysis without four isolated tribes, and a 2013 Institute of Medicine report.
grade: D
src: YT BIwhVshA1Nk; He 2013 BMJ; IOM 2013
counter: Salt reduction lowers blood pressure most in people with hypertension, and lower intake cut cardiovascular events in a large RCT (SSaSS, Neal 2021 N Engl J Med). Japan's high salt intake went with very high stroke rates.
safety: none
when: eat | myth
aud: advanced
tier: drop
note: Don't ship general "salt is fine" claims. Salt during fasting (CGF-00-008, YT-01-011) is a separate, defensible context.

### YT-03-002
claim: Most people eat less magnesium than recommended. Refining strips most of it: white flour loses ~80% of wheat's magnesium, polished rice ~80%, cornstarch ~97%.
why: Whole grains, nuts, legumes, dark chocolate, tofu and seafood are rich sources. Processing, softened water, alcohol, diuretics and gut disease all lower intake or raise losses.
grade: B
src: YT lbZz1yCdA8U
counter: In US surveys about half of adults fall below the average requirement. Fung's 50–85% is the upper range; soil-depletion claims are contested.
safety: none
when: eat | between
aud: all
tier: tail

### YT-03-003
claim: If you supplement magnesium, choose citrate or glycinate; magnesium oxide is poorly absorbed and mostly acts as a laxative.
why: Organic magnesium salts are absorbed several times better; oxide carries a lot of elemental magnesium but little enters the blood.
do: With kidney disease, don't take magnesium supplements without your doctor: impaired kidneys can't clear the excess.
grade: B
src: YT lbZz1yCdA8U; Firoz & Graber 2001 Magnes Res
safety: caution
when: long | symptom:cramps | between
aud: all
tier: core
note: Fung also lists carbonate among the "organic" forms; it's inorganic. Omit it.

### YT-03-004
claim: Muscle cramps, twitches, constipation and fatigue can signal low magnesium, even when blood tests look normal.
why: Only about 1% of the body's magnesium is in the blood; most is in bone and muscle, so a blood test can miss a deficit.
grade: B
src: YT lbZz1yCdA8U; CGF ch14
safety: none
when: symptom:cramps | symptom:fatigue | long
aud: all

### YT-03-005
claim: Eat carbohydrates last. Same meal, carbs eaten after protein and vegetables: blood sugar and insulin rose roughly 40–50% less.
why: Protein, fat and fiber eaten first slow stomach emptying and prime gut hormones, so starch arrives later and gentler.
do: Start with protein and vegetables; leave bread, rice or potato for the end, ideally 10 minutes later. Ask restaurants to bring bread with the meal.
grade: B
src: YT o8TeVf6rR7k; Shukla 2015 Diabetes Care (PMID 26106234); Shukla 2019 Diabetes Obes Metab (PMID 30101510)
counter: Small crossover studies (n≈11–16); long-term outcome data are lacking.
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all, t2d
tier: core
note: VERIFIED: prediabetes study, glucose peaks >40% lower, iAUC −38.8% with protein/vegetables first.

### YT-03-006
claim: Eating carbs first can cause a sugar crash a few hours later: in one study a prediabetic participant became shaky and sweaty.
why: A fast glucose spike triggers a large insulin surge that overshoots, dropping blood sugar below baseline around three hours after eating.
grade: B
src: YT o8TeVf6rR7k; Shukla 2019 Diabetes Obes Metab (PMID 30101510)
safety: none
when: eat | hunger
aud: all

### YT-03-007
claim: Adding acid to starch blunts its blood-sugar spike: vinegar, lemon juice, or pickled and fermented foods alongside bread or rice.
why: Acid inactivates saliva's starch-digesting enzyme, which does much of the work in the stomach. Vinegar with bread lowered glucose and insulin dose-dependently and increased fullness.
do: Avoid "naked carbs": pair starch with vinegar dressing, pickles, lemon, protein or fat.
grade: B
src: YT Et0GY94ebBQ; Östman 2005 Eur J Clin Nutr; Sugiyama 2003 Eur J Clin Nutr
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all
tier: core

### YT-03-008
claim: Plateau checklist beyond diet: cut all sweeteners, sleep enough, and actively manage stress.
why: Sweeteners keep cravings alive; short sleep increases appetite and intake; chronic stress raises cortisol, which promotes weight gain and stress eating.
do: Treat sleep and stress like part of the protocol: regular bedtime, daily movement, meditation, yoga or time with people.
grade: B
src: YT dRO5xSx1flw; Al Khatib 2017 Eur J Clin Nutr
safety: none
when: plateau | between
aud: all
tier: core

### YT-03-011
claim: Your body defends a set weight. Overfed people burned ~500 kcal/day more; after losing 10%, they burned 300–400 kcal/day less.
why: Like a thermostat, the body adjusts energy use to resist change in either direction. This is why calorie cutting alone tends to stall.
grade: A
src: YT RL8x7FTSo-Y; Leibel 1995 N Engl J Med
counter: Fung's view that insulin sets the "thermostat" is a hypothesis. Leptin and brain circuits are the best-established regulators.
safety: none
when: plateau | myth
aud: all
tier: core

### YT-03-013
claim: Many "breakfast foods" are dessert in disguise: cereals, muffins, pastries, fruit-on-the-bottom yogurt, breakfast bars, jam on white toast.
why: Low-fat breakfast products often replaced fat with sugar. Refined carbs spike glucose and insulin, leaving you ravenous by mid-morning.
do: If you eat breakfast, choose eggs, meat, plain yogurt or slow-cooked whole oats, or simply delay your first meal.
grade: B
src: YT WBEMhsx7hGg
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all

### YT-03-014
claim: Sugar (sucrose, high-fructose corn syrup) is worse than starch. Its fructose half is processed by the liver, where excess becomes liver fat.
why: Unlike glucose, fructose bypasses most cells and is converted to fat in the liver when intake is high, driving fatty liver and insulin resistance.
grade: B
src: YT WBEMhsx7hGg; Stanhope 2009 J Clin Invest

### YT-03-015
claim: Breakfast can wait. Around 4–5 a.m. your body releases hormones that put fuel into the blood, so you're already fueled when you wake.
why: Growth hormone, cortisol and noradrenaline mobilize stored energy before waking; that's why morning hunger is typically lowest.
do: Eat when you're hungry, not because it's morning. 7 a.m., 9 a.m. or noon all work.
grade: B

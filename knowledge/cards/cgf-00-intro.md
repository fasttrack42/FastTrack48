# CGF: Introduction (Fung) + "Not Just Another F-Word" (Moore)

### CGF-00-001
claim: In type 2 diabetes insulin is usually high, not low; the problem is that cells resist it.
why: Type 1 destroys insulin-making cells, so insulin is missing. Type 2 starts with resistance: the pancreas over-produces insulin to force sugar into unresponsive cells.
grade: A
src: CGF intro
safety: none
when: onboarding | myth
aud: t2d
tier: core

### CGF-00-002
claim: Prescribed insulin lowers blood sugar but reliably adds weight, which tends to deepen type 2 diabetes over time.
why: Insulin is the body's main storage signal. More of it pushes energy into fat, and extra weight raises resistance, so doses keep climbing.
do: If you take insulin, never adjust it yourself; fasting with your doctor can lower the dose you need.
grade: A
src: CGF intro; UKPDS 1998 Lancet
counter: Weight gain from insulin is well documented. "Treating T2D with insulin is exactly wrong" is Fung's view; insulin is still needed in many cases.
safety: caution
when: onboarding
aud: t2d, meds
tier: core

### CGF-00-003
claim: Constantly high insulin may itself drive insulin resistance, the way any constant drug exposure builds tolerance.
why: Cells exposed to persistently high insulin down-regulate their response. Lowering insulin through fasting periods gives them a chance to resensitize.
grade: B
src: CGF intro; Del Prato 1994 Diabetologia (PMID 7851681)
counter: Shown in clamp studies over days; it is one cause among several (fat in liver and muscle, genetics, inactivity).
safety: none
when: stage:54-72 | myth
aud: all, t2d
tier: core
note: VERIFIED citation (PubMed).

### CGF-00-004
claim: Type 2 diabetes can often be put into remission through diet. It is not necessarily a lifelong, progressive disease.
why: When liver and pancreas fat fall with sustained weight loss, insulin secretion and sensitivity can recover, especially early in the disease.
do: Remission is achievable; track it with your doctor through HbA1c, not by stopping medication on your own.
grade: A
src: CGF intro; Lean 2018 Lancet (DiRECT)
counter: DiRECT used a low-calorie diet, not fasting. Fasting-specific evidence is case series (Furmli 2018 BMJ Case Rep). Remission is less likely after many years of disease.
safety: caution
when: onboarding
aud: t2d
tier: core

### CGF-00-005
claim: Fasting is the simplest way to lower insulin. It needs no special foods, recipes or shopping.
why: Every food raises insulin to some degree. Not eating is the one approach that drops it for everyone, whatever their diet.
grade: D
src: CGF intro; YT tIuj-oMN-Fk
safety: none
when: plan | onboarding
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-00-007
claim: Diet sodas during a fast tend to make hunger and cravings worse, not better.
why: Intense sweetness without energy may keep appetite and food reward primed. Many fasters report stronger cravings with sweeteners.
do: Drink water, tea instead of sweetened drinks.
grade: D
src: CGF intro
counter: Human trials on sweeteners and appetite are mixed. This is mainly clinical experience.
safety: none
when: fluids | hunger
aud: all
tier: tail
note: Superseded by CGF-09-006 (better evidence); keep as anecdotal support.

### CGF-00-008
claim: Fatigue and light-headedness while fasting are often low salt, not low energy.
why: Falling insulin makes the kidneys excrete sodium and water ("natriuresis of fasting"). Low sodium causes fatigue, dizziness and headache.
do: Add a pinch of salt to a glass of water.
grade: B
src: CGF intro; Spark 1975 N Engl J Med (PMID 165411)
safety: caution
when: symptom:fatigue | symptom:dizzy | stage:24-48
aud: all
tier: core
note: VERIFIED: natriuresis of fasting (Spark 1975 NEJM; Boulter 1974 JCEM PMID 4812620).

### CGF-00-009
claim: Hunger after a long fast can trigger heavy overeating. The first meal deserves a plan.
why: Pent-up hunger plus a sudden large meal overloads a gut that has been resting, causing pain and undoing the calm the fast created.
do: Break a fast with a small meal, wait 30–60 minutes, then eat normally.
grade: D
src: CGF intro
safety: none
when: break
aud: beginner
tier: tail
note: Superseded by CGF-15-004 (canonical break-fast card).

### CGF-00-010
claim: Day 2 is commonly the hardest. Hunger often fades after it rather than building.
why: Ghrelin and hunger follow learned meal times and peak early. As ketones rise around days 2–3, appetite usually drops.
do: If day 2 is rough, expect it to ease. Don't judge the whole fast by its hardest day.
grade: B
src: CGF intro; CGF ch9
counter: Mostly self-reported. Ghrelin data are covered in ch9 cards.
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | hunger
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-00-011
claim: Already eating low-carb makes fasting markedly easier, because the body is used to running on fat.
why: Glycogen holds roughly 2,000 kcal; even lean bodies store tens of thousands of kcal as fat. Fat-adapted people switch fuels without a hunger spike.
do: Before trying longer fasts, a few weeks of lower-carb eating can soften the transition.
grade: B
src: CGF intro
counter: Fung also uses fasting successfully in people who aren't keto-adapted. Adaptation helps, but isn't required.
safety: none
when: plan | long
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-00-012
claim: Ketogenic eating often blunts appetite so much that people skip meals without trying.
why: Ketones and steady blood sugar suppress hunger hormones; fewer insulin swings mean fewer rebound cravings.
grade: A
src: CGF intro; Gibson 2015 Obes Rev
safety: none
when: eat | between
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-00-013
claim: Stop the fast if you feel truly unwell: energy gone and not returning, faint, or ravenous beyond normal hunger.
why: Normal hunger comes in waves and passes. Persistent weakness or feeling "not yourself" means the fast isn't right for you today.
do: End it, eat something, and try again in a week. Stopping isn't failing.
grade: D
src: CGF intro
safety: stop
when: start | stage:24-48 | long
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-00-014
claim: Long fasts often fail during travel, stress or celebrations. Plan them around those events.
why: Fasting is itself a stressor. On top of other stress it shifts from beneficial (hormetic) to draining, and hunger rises.
do: Don't start a multi-day fast just before travel, a wedding or a demanding week. Short fasts are fine.
grade: D
src: CGF intro
safety: none
when: plan | long
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-00-015
claim: Most of the scale drop in a week-long fast is water and stored glycogen, not fat.
why: Each gram of glycogen is stored with about 3 g of water. Emptying glycogen sheds 1–2 kg quickly, and much of it returns on refeeding.
do: Judge long fasts by the weight still off a few weeks later, not by the last day's number.
grade: A
src: CGF intro
safety: none
when: long | break | plateau
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-00-016
claim: Body scans during a fast can show "muscle loss" that is really water and glycogen, which returns after eating.
why: DXA counts water and glycogen as lean mass. In Moore's 28-day fast, scanned lean loss had fully recovered after two weeks of eating.
grade: B
src: CGF intro
counter: n=1. Fung attributed it to organ fat; water/glycogen is the more standard explanation. Some real protein loss does occur, mostly early in a fast.
safety: none
when: myth | long
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-00-017
claim: During long fasts blood sugar typically settles around 60–75 mg/dL without symptoms. Readings in the 50s with weakness mean break the fast.
why: The liver keeps glucose low but steady and the brain shifts to ketones. Symptomatic lows mean that balance is failing.
do: If you're shaky, sweaty, confused or can barely stand, eat now, and measure if you have a meter.
grade: B
src: CGF intro; Cahill 2006 Annu Rev Nutr
safety: stop
when: long | stage:72+ | symptom:dizzy
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-00-018
claim: Most doctors receive only a few hours of nutrition training across years of medical education.
why: Fung estimates about four hours in nine years. US surveys show most schools fall below the recommended 25 hours.
grade: B
src: CGF intro; Adams 2010 Acad Med
safety: none
when: history
aud: all
tier: drop
note: true but not user-actionable; keep as context only.

### CGF-00-019
claim: Prolonged fasting can temporarily raise LDL cholesterol in lean people, even though it improves most metabolic markers.
why: Fat mobilized from stores circulates as lipoproteins. Lean fasters show the largest rise, which reverses after refeeding.
grade: B
src: Sävendahl & Underwood 1999 J Nutr (PMID 10539776)
counter: Moore's n=1 showed a large LDL drop (he was overweight). Results depend on body fat and timing of the test.
safety: none
when: long | myth
aud: advanced
tier: tail
note: VERIFIED title: fasting increases total cholesterol, LDL, apoB in healthy nonobese humans.

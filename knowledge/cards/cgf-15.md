# CGF ch15: Fasting Tips and FAQs

### CGF-15-001
claim: Fung's single most important tip: fit fasting into your life, not your life into fasting.
why: Adherence decides results. Forcing fasts through holidays, weddings and trips breeds resentment and quitting; flexible scheduling lasts.
do: Skip fasting on celebration days without guilt; resume your usual schedule the day after.
grade: D
src: CGF ch15
safety: none
when: plan | between | fastday
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-15-003
claim: Big dinner tonight? Skip breakfast and lunch. Fasting before a social meal is the easiest way to fit both.
why: Breakfast is rarely social, and lunch on workdays is easy to work through; a planned feast then lands on a day that's mostly fasted.

### CGF-15-004
claim: Break a fast gently: a small snack first, then the main meal 30–60 minutes later. The longer the fast, the gentler the restart.
why: Pent-up appetite is mostly psychological; a pause lets hunger waves pass and spares a rested gut from overload and cramping.
do: After fasts under 24 h, no special care is needed. For longer ones, prepare the small dish in advance so convenience food doesn't win.
grade: D
src: CGF ch15; CGF intro
safety: none
when: break
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-15-005
claim: Good first bites: a few nuts, a spoon of nut butter, a small salad, raw vegetables with olive oil, vegetable soup, or a little meat.
why: Small, fatty or fibrous portions satisfy without a big glucose and insulin spike, easing the gut back to work.
do: Eat slowly, chew thoroughly, and drink a tall glass of water before and after.
grade: D
src: CGF ch15
counter: Fung's later advice (YT PVJCdREj4Og) flags nuts, seeds, nut butters, raw vegetables, eggs and dairy as the most common post-fast troublemakers; prefer YT-02-001.
safety: none
when: break
aud: all
tier: tail
note: CONFLICT with newer Fung advice; superseded by YT-02-001.

### CGF-15-007

### CGF-15-008
claim: A glass of water before a meal reduces how much you eat.
why: Water stretches the stomach and dampens appetite; in a 12-week trial, pre-meal water increased weight loss in older adults.
do: Drink a full glass about 30 minutes before your first meal and before dinner.
grade: B
src: CGF ch15; Dennis 2010 Obesity
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-15-010

### CGF-15-013
claim: Dizziness while fasting usually means low water and salt, or, if you take blood-pressure pills, pressure dropping too low.
why: Falling insulin flushes sodium and water; antihypertensives then overshoot. Standing up quickly makes it worse.
do: Drink water with a pinch of salt, rise slowly, and ask your doctor about BP medication if it recurs.
grade: B
src: CGF ch15
safety: caution
when: symptom:dizzy | stage:24-48 | long
aud: all, meds
tier: core

### CGF-15-014
claim: Headaches in your first few fasts are common and usually temporary, often from the drop in salt intake.
why: Going from a salty diet to almost no salt changes fluid balance; the body adapts within a few fasts.
do: Take extra salt in water or drink mineral water.
grade: D
src: CGF ch15

### CGF-15-015
claim: Mild tiredness early on is common, but persistent fatigue is not normal fasting. Stop and see your doctor.
why: Most people feel energized by rising noradrenaline. Lasting exhaustion points to dehydration, low salt, low blood sugar or another problem.

### CGF-15-016
claim: Red flags that mean stop: persistent nausea, vomiting, dizziness, fatigue, very high or low blood sugar, or lethargy. Hunger and constipation are normal.
why: These signs fall outside the normal adaptation to fasting and can signal dehydration, electrolyte trouble or medication problems.
do: Stop the fast and contact your doctor.
grade: A
src: CGF ch15
safety: stop

### CGF-15-017
claim: If you keep having low blood sugar on fasting days, your medication dose is too high. That's not the fasting failing.
why: Diabetes drugs are dosed for eating days. Repeated lows mean the dose needs reducing, which only your doctor should do.
do: Record the readings and times, and contact your doctor before the next fast.
grade: A
src: CGF ch15
safety: stop
when: start | between
aud: t2d, meds
tier: core

### CGF-15-018
claim: On blood-pressure medication, check your blood pressure at home during fasting periods.
why: Fasting lowers pressure by shedding salt and water; combined with medication, it can fall too low.

### CGF-15-019
claim: High morning blood sugar during a multi-day fast isn't a failure: it's stored sugar the liver is releasing, the dawn phenomenon.
why: Before waking and during fasting, glucagon, cortisol, adrenaline and GH push liver glucose out. With insulin resistance, blood sugar shows it.
do: Look at the trend over weeks, not one reading. It eases as stored sugar is cleared.
grade: B
src: CGF ch15
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | stage:54-72 | long
aud: t2d
tier: core

### CGF-15-020
claim: You can exercise while fasting. A 3.5-day fast didn't reduce strength, anaerobic capacity or endurance in one study.
why: Glycogen covers about a day of needs and fat supplies the rest; rising noradrenaline supports effort.
do: Keep your usual workouts; scale back if you feel faint, and expect a ~2-week dip if you're newly fat-adapting.
grade: B
src: CGF ch15; Knapik 1987 Eur J Appl Physiol
counter: Fung says fasting lets you "build muscle faster" via GH; there's no evidence for that. Fasting GH doesn't act as an anabolic signal.
safety: none
when: between | stage:12-20 | stage:24-48 | myth
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-15-021
claim: Training in a fasted state builds more fat-burning machinery in muscle.
why: Low glycogen during exercise increases fat-oxidation proteins and intramuscular fat use; useful for endurance athletes.
grade: B
src: CGF ch15; Van Proeyen 2011 J Appl Physiol
counter: Performance gains in competition aren't consistently shown; high-intensity work may suffer.
safety: none
when: between
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### CGF-15-022
claim: People sometimes feel "hangry" when hungry, but expectation plays a large part. Regular fasters rarely report irritability.
why: Fung suggests people act out the irritability they expect; framing fasting as normal reduces it.
grade: D
src: CGF ch15
counter: Hunger is linked to anger and irritability in daily-life studies (Swami 2022 PLoS One). It's real, but usually mild and fades with practice.

### CGF-15-023
claim: About two-thirds of the year's average weight gain happens in the six weeks from late November to New Year.
why: Holiday feasting concentrates the surplus; the weight often isn't lost afterward, so it accumulates year after year.
do: Plan a few fasts in early January. Feasts are fine when they're followed by fasts.
grade: B
src: CGF ch15; Yanovski 2000 N Engl J Med
safety: none
when: between | fastday
aud: all

### CGF-15-024
claim: Adjust your fasting by results and by season: shorter, more frequent fasts may suit summer, longer and less frequent ones winter.
why: Goals differ (a few kilos vs severe diabetes), and stalls mean the stimulus needs to change. Nothing about the schedule is fixed.
grade: D

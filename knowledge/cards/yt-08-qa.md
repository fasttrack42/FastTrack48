### YT-08-003
claim: Feeling cold while fasting is common early on and usually fades as your body gets better at burning fat. Layer up, drink hot, move.
why: New fasters can lag in switching to fat fuel; movement uses circulating fuel and generates heat. With consistency many go from too cold to warm.
do: If you're cold all the time even when not fasting, or it's severe, ask your doctor to check thyroid function.
grade: D
src: YT OcPvjyLd1g4
counter: Feeling cold can also signal under-eating (low energy availability) or hypothyroidism.
safety: none

tier: core

### YT-08-006
claim: The premenstrual week is the hardest time to fast; insulin sensitivity dips and cravings rise. Shorten fasts and avoid sweeteners or extra cream between meals.
why: Insulin sensitivity is lower in the luteal phase; "feeding" cravings with sweet-tasting aids tends to escalate into eating.
do: Fasting usually gets easier again from day 2–3 of your period through ovulation.
grade: B
src: YT OcPvjyLd1g4
safety: none
when: plan | hunger
aud: women
tier: core

### YT-08-007
claim: The Fasting Method treats 16:8 as maintenance. For fat loss they use 24–48-hour fasts, two or three times a week.
why: Coaches find insulin falls substantially only after about 24 hours; daily 16:8 prevents things worsening but rarely reverses them on its own.
grade: D
src: YT OcPvjyLd1g4 (Ramos)
counter: Consistent with RCTs showing modest weight loss with time-restricted eating alone (SCI-12-001). The "24-hour insulin threshold" is clinical impression.
safety: none
when: plan | plateau
aud: all
tier: core

why: Like moving up to heavier weights, progress sticks when each level is mastered first; jumping ahead invites the fast-then-binge cycle.

why: Refined carbs set up a hunger rebound, and chronic under-eating makes the body push hard for food; both undermine the next fast.

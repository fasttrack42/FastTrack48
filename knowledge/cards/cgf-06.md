# CGF ch6: Fasting for Type 2 Diabetes

### CGF-06-001
claim: More than half of US adults have diabetes or prediabetes, and most people with prediabetes don't know it.
why: In 2012, 14% had diabetes and 38% prediabetes. Prediabetes rarely causes symptoms, so it goes unnoticed for years.
do: Ask for an HbA1c or fasting glucose test at your next check-up, especially if you carry weight around the waist.
grade: A
src: CGF ch6; Menke 2015 JAMA
safety: none
when: onboarding
aud: all
tier: core

### CGF-06-002
claim: Before insulin existed, the best diabetes treatment was supervised fasting followed by a very low-carb diet.
why: Allen and Joslin (1915–1921) found that fasting cleared sugar from the urine of many patients, likely those with type 2.
grade: B
src: CGF ch6; Allen 1915 Am J Med Sci; Joslin 1916 Can Med Assoc J; Mazur 2011 Nutr J
counter: Children with type 1 diabetes often died of starvation on it, which is why fasting is unsafe for type 1 and underweight people.
safety: none
when: history
aud: t2d
tier: tail

### CGF-06-003
claim: Diabetes deaths fell sharply during wartime food rationing in both world wars, and rose again once rationing ended.
why: Sustained, population-wide calorie restriction lowered blood sugar and weight; the rebound followed normal eating, even after insulin arrived.
grade: B
src: CGF ch6; Cleave 1974 The Saccharine Disease
counter: Ecological data, confounded by wartime changes in activity, survival and record-keeping.
safety: none
when: history
aud: t2d
tier: tail

### CGF-06-004
claim: Fat in the liver, pancreas and around organs drives type 2 diabetes far more than fat under the skin. Fasting tends to clear it first.
why: Fat in the liver causes insulin resistance there; fat in the pancreas impairs insulin release. Losing it can restore normal glucose control.
grade: A
src: CGF ch6; Lim 2011 Diabetologia; Taylor 2013 Diabetes Care
safety: none
when: stage:54-72 | onboarding
aud: t2d, all
tier: core
note: Taylor's "twin cycle" (liver + pancreas fat) is the best-supported mechanism behind remission.

### CGF-06-005
claim: Fung pictures insulin resistance as an overfull subway train: the cell is already stuffed with fuel, so insulin can't push more sugar in.
why: In his model, excess stored energy overflows into the blood. The cure is to empty the cell (eat less, burn more), not to push harder with more insulin.
grade: D
src: CGF ch6
counter: The analogy matches the lipid-overload model (fat in liver and muscle blocks insulin signaling) rather than glucose literally overfilling cells.
safety: none
when: stage:54-72 | onboarding
aud: t2d
tier: tail

### CGF-06-006
claim: Fasting while on diabetes medication without a plan can cause dangerous low blood sugar: shaking, sweating, confusion, even loss of consciousness.
why: Most diabetes drugs lower sugar on the assumption that you'll eat. Remove the food and the same dose overshoots.
do: See your doctor before fasting on insulin or diabetes pills. Expect your doses to change on fasting days.
grade: A
src: CGF ch6
safety: stop
when: onboarding | plan | start
aud: t2d, meds
tier: core

### CGF-06-007
claim: If you fast while on glucose-lowering drugs, check your blood sugar 2–4 times a day, on fasting and eating days.
why: Changes come fast and vary by drug; frequent checks let you and your doctor catch lows early and adjust doses.
do: Treat a low (under ~4 mmol/L, 70 mg/dL) with sugar right away, even if it ends the fast, and tell your doctor.
grade: A
src: CGF ch6
safety: stop
when: start | stage:12-20 | stage:24-48
aud: t2d, meds
tier: core

### CGF-06-008
claim: Fung's clinic prefers slightly high sugar on fasting days to any risk of a low: about 8–10 mmol/L (145–180 mg/dL) while on medication.
why: Short-term mild highs are harmless while fasting brings sugar down; a low is acutely dangerous and breaks the fast.
do: Agree target ranges and a "less medication on fast days" plan with your own doctor. Don't adopt these numbers alone.
grade: D
src: CGF ch6
safety: caution
when: plan | start
aud: t2d, meds
tier: core

### CGF-06-009
claim: If you aren't on glucose-lowering medication, there's no need to monitor blood sugar during fasting; it stays in the normal range.
why: Without drugs pushing sugar down, the liver keeps glucose steady through glycogen and gluconeogenesis.
grade: A
src: CGF ch6
safety: none
when: start | myth
aud: all
tier: tail

### CGF-06-010
claim: A fast that ends early still counts. Stopping at 22 of 24 planned hours is 22 hours of benefit, not a failure.
why: Benefits accumulate hour by hour. Treating short attempts as wins builds the confidence that makes longer fasts possible.
do: End when you need to, log what you did, and try again; each attempt gets easier.
grade: D
src: CGF ch6 (Megan)
safety: none
when: break | between
aud: beginner
tier: core
note: Supports the app's non-punitive tone (red countdowns already removed).

### CGF-06-011
claim: Staying busy is one of the most effective ways to get through early fasts.
why: Hunger is largely triggered by cues and boredom. Engaged attention leaves less room for food thoughts.
do: Schedule fasts on work days rather than idle days; plan an activity for the usual mealtime.
grade: D
src: CGF ch6 (Megan); CGF ch9
safety: none
when: hunger | plan
aud: beginner
tier: core

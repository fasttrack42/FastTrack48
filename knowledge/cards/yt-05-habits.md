### YT-05-001
claim: Snacking became near-universal (97% of Americans by 2006) and snacks' share of daily calories grew. Most snacks are salty foods and desserts.
why: Meals barely shrink to compensate, so snacks add energy; they're mostly shelf-stable, ultra-processed foods rather than fruit, vegetables or protein.
grade: B
src: YT _xgEG6C_Bdk; Piernas & Popkin 2010 J Nutr
safety: none
when: between | eat
aud: all
tier: tail

### YT-05-002
claim: Snacks work like appetizers: small tasty bites that stoke appetite and keep food on your mind all day.
why: Each snack restarts insulin and the cephalic response; frequent eating also means more thinking about food.
do: Treat "no snacks" as the first and most powerful fasting step. If you're not hungry, don't eat.
grade: D
src: YT _xgEG6C_Bdk; YT yNRARYt0nkg
safety: none
when: between | hunger
aud: all
tier: core

### YT-05-003
claim: Skipping breakfast doesn't make you eat more overall. In trials, breakfast eaters consumed ~260 kcal more per day and weren't lighter.
why: The meal after a skipped breakfast grows only slightly; total daily intake falls. A meta-analysis of RCTs found no weight benefit from eating breakfast.
grade: A
src: YT _xgEG6C_Bdk; Sievert 2019 BMJ
safety: none
when: myth | plan
aud: all
tier: core

### YT-05-004
claim: IGF-1, a growth signal linked to cancer and aging, follows a U-shaped curve: both very high and very low levels are linked to higher mortality.
why: High IGF-1 drives cell growth (associated with several cancers); very low levels reflect malnutrition. Dairy and animal protein raise it most.
grade: B
src: YT rCVJOGdA_Rs; Burgers 2011 J Clin Endocrinol Metab
counter: Observational associations; causation and optimal ranges are unsettled.
safety: none
when: stage:72+ | eat
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### YT-05-005
claim: Ordinary daily fasting barely moves IGF-1. It falls when intake drops below about half of normal, as on full fast days or multi-day fasts.
why: A meta-analysis found IGF-1 decreased only in fasting regimens with severe calorie restriction; occasional full fast days (as in many religions) supply that dip.
grade: B
src: YT rCVJOGdA_Rs
counter: Fung's figures for this meta-analysis are unverified.
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | stage:72+ | fastday
aud: advanced
tier: tail

### YT-05-006
claim: Sleeping about an hour more cut food intake by ~270 kcal a day in short sleepers, and they lost weight without any diet advice.
why: In a 2022 RCT, adults sleeping under 6.5 h extended sleep by ~1.2 h; intake fell while energy expenditure didn't change.
do: If you sleep under 6.5 hours, an earlier bedtime may be the easiest weight lever you have.
grade: A
src: YT wacFeIqFQ_8; Tasali 2022 JAMA Intern Med
safety: none
when: plateau | between | symptom:insomnia
aud: all
tier: core

### YT-05-007
claim: Eating out of sync with your body clock raises the blood sugar and insulin response to the same meal and lowers leptin, the fullness hormone.
why: In circadian-misalignment experiments, identical meals produced higher glucose and insulin, and lower leptin, which means more hunger and more storage.
grade: A
src: YT wacFeIqFQ_8; Scheer 2009 Proc Natl Acad Sci USA
safety: none
when: eat | plan
aud: all
tier: tail
note: Supports CGF-12-006 and CGF-12-008.

### YT-05-008
claim: Eat a simple home-cooked meal, low in sugar and starch, the night before a fast. Restaurant meals make the next day harder.
why: Sugar and starch make you retain water; when insulin falls you shed it quickly, losing electrolytes, which causes headache, nausea and fatigue.
grade: D
src: YT TlQoAZrNGnE (Megan Ramos)
safety: none
when: start | plan
aud: all
tier: core

### YT-05-009
claim: Set a reminder every couple of hours on fasting days to drink: water with a pinch of salt or pickle juice.
why: Most of our water and electrolytes normally come with food; new fasters lose extra fluid as insulin drops, and it's easy to forget to drink.
grade: D
src: YT TlQoAZrNGnE
safety: none
when: start | fluids | stage:12-20

### YT-05-010
claim: Megan Ramos recommends an Epsom-salt bath or foot soak the night before a fast to top up magnesium and calm fasting jitters.
why: Her view: noradrenaline rises during fasting and magnesium counters the wired feeling; she considers skin absorption better than pills for people with gut issues.
grade: D
src: YT TlQoAZrNGnE
counter: Fung says oral citrate or glycinate is better studied and skin absorption is uncertain (YT lbZz1yCdA8U; Gröber 2017 Nutrients). The bath relaxes either way.

### YT-05-011
claim: Getting back on track after a break or a stressful period: start with three meals and no snacks, then drop meals one at a time.
why: Removing snacks lowers insulin with almost no hunger cost; regaining appetite control first makes the return to longer fasts easier.
grade: D
src: YT yNRARYt0nkg (Megan Ramos)
safety: none

### YT-05-012
claim: If stress makes you eat, choose natural fats and protein (olives, avocado, eggs, fatty meat) over chips and sweets.
why: Fat and protein satisfy without blood-sugar swings; processed carbs bring water retention, brain fog, poor sleep and higher sugars that last for days.
do: Before eating, ask yourself what this food will do to you in a few hours.

claim: Keep your eating window short: a meal that stretches into hours of grazing is snacking in disguise. Ramos's group challenge capped meals at 90 minutes.
why: Grazing repeatedly raises insulin; a defined start and end keeps the fasted hours truly fasted.

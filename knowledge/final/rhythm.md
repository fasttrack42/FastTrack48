# Final cards: Rhythm & progress: choosing a schedule, fitting it into life, plateaus, measuring progress, myths.

### FC-090
title: Start by cutting snacks
claim: The easiest first fast: three meals, nothing in between. Most adults now eat 5–6 times a day, leaving almost no low-insulin time.
why: Each snack raises insulin again and works like an appetizer, stoking appetite. Removing snacks restores a daily fat-burning window at almost no hunger cost.
do: Then drop meals one at a time. The same path works for restarting after a break.
grade: B
safety: none
when: between | onboarding | plan
aud: all, beginner
from: CGF-01-003, YT-05-002, YT-05-001, YT-05-011, YT-02-016, SCI-92-008

### FC-091
title: The best schedule is repeatable
claim: There's no single best fasting schedule. The right one is the one you can keep, and you can change it anytime.
why: Short daily fasts (16:8, one meal a day) are easiest to sustain; longer, less frequent fasts act faster on blood sugar and liver fat but are harder socially.
do: Build a routine around a daily window; add longer fasts in quiet weeks.
grade: D
safety: none
when: plan | onboarding
aud: all
from: CGF-12-001, YT-01-005, CGF-12-003, YT-01-006

### FC-092
title: An eating window alone is modest
claim: In large trials, a 16:8 window alone gave little extra weight loss. Benefits come mostly from eating less and better within it.
why: When calories were matched, time-restricted eating did no better than ordinary calorie restriction. Fung's Fasting Method team treats 16:8 as maintenance and uses longer fasts for fat loss.
do: Use the window as structure, then fill it with whole, protein-rich food and no grazing.
grade: A
counter: Early-day eating windows improved insulin sensitivity and blood pressure even without weight loss.
safety: none
when: plan | plateau
aud: all
from: SCI-12-001, CGF-12-004, YT-08-007

### FC-093
title: The 24-hour fast
claim: Dinner to dinner: you still eat one meal that day, so family dinners and food-needing medicines stay possible.
why: Skipping breakfast and lunch gives a full day of low insulin with little disruption. Two or three a week is a common rhythm.
grade: D
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all
from: CGF-13-002, CGF-16-006

### FC-094
title: The 36- and 42-hour fasts
claim: A 36-hour fast skips one whole day, dinner to breakfast two days later; skip that breakfast too and it's 42 hours.
why: It spans two nights of sleep. Fung's clinic uses these three times a week for type 2 diabetes, under supervision, then eases off.
grade: D
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all, t2d
from: CGF-13-003, CGF-16-007, YT-01-007

### FC-095
title: 5:2 and alternate days
claim: 5:2 means two low days (~500–600 kcal) a week. Alternate-day fasting repeats that every other day; both work about as well as daily dieting.
why: A small allowance makes fast days easier. In trials, weight loss matched daily calorie cutting; insulin sometimes improved more, but dropout was higher.
grade: A
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all
from: CGF-13-004, CGF-13-005, CGF-05-007

### FC-096
title: Skip breakfast or dinner?
claim: Skipping breakfast is easiest; skipping dinner is most effective. Choose by what your day allows.
why: Breakfast is small, little-hungry and rarely social. Dinner is the biggest meal, eaten when insulin responses run highest, but it's the social one.
grade: D
safety: none
when: plan
aud: all
from: YT-04-005

### FC-100
title: Fit fasting into your life
claim: Fit fasting into your life, not your life into fasting. Feast at celebrations, then fast in the days after.
why: Humans have always paired feasts with fasts. Much of the year's weight gain lands in the winter holidays; balance over weeks matters more than one meal.
do: Big dinner tonight? Skip breakfast and lunch. Fast harder on busy weekdays, lighter on family weekends.
grade: B
safety: none
when: between | plan | fastday
aud: all
from: CGF-15-001, CGF-04-003, CGF-04-004, CGF-15-003, CGF-15-023

### FC-104
title: Train near the end
claim: A good time to exercise is toward the end of a fast, then break it with a protein-rich meal.
why: Late in a fast noradrenaline is high and fat-burning is running. A 3.5-day fast didn't reduce strength or endurance in one study.
do: Example: fast from dinner, train at noon, eat lunch after. Scale back if you feel faint.
grade: B
safety: none
when: between | stage:12-20 | stage:20-24
aud: all
from: YT-02-011, CGF-15-020

### FC-106
title: Bones need load
claim: Bones strengthen only under load. Walking, stairs and resistance training protect them far more than calcium pills.
why: Bone remodels in response to mechanical stress; astronauts lose bone despite eating well. Daily movement matters as much as gym sessions.
grade: A
safety: none
when: between | long
aud: all, women
from: YT-07-006

### FC-107
title: Treatment, then maintenance
claim: Intensive fasting can be a time-limited treatment. Once you reach your goal, maintenance is much lighter.
why: Clinics run therapeutic protocols for months, then step down to daily eating windows plus occasional longer fasts after holidays.
grade: D
safety: none
when: between | plan
aud: all
from: YT-08-008

### FC-110
title: Breaking a plateau
claim: Plateaus are normal. Change the stimulus: a longer fast, a different schedule, or a stricter fast without sweeteners.
why: Any routine reaches balance as weight lost on fasting days matches weight regained. A new pattern restarts progress.
do: Try 24 → 36 → 48 hours, one meal a day, or an occasional longer fast. Check snacking and sleep too.
grade: D
safety: none
when: plateau
aud: all
from: CGF-05-014, CGF-04-006, CGF-15-024, YT-08-013

### FC-111
title: Measure more than weight
claim: When the scale stalls, measure your waist or take a monthly photo. Aim to keep your waist under half your height.
why: Water shifts hide fat loss for weeks. Waist size tracks the visceral fat most tied to diabetes and heart disease.
grade: A
safety: none
when: plateau | between
aud: all
from: CGF-13-007, YT-01-012, YT-04-014

### FC-112
title: Your body defends its weight
claim: After weight loss, appetite rises about 100 kcal a day per kilo lost, and metabolism slows. Regain is biology, not weak willpower.
why: The body defends a set weight like a thermostat. Most calorie-cutting diets plateau and rebound; Biggest Loser contestants burned ~500 kcal/day less, six years on.
do: Expect stronger hunger as you lose; regular fasting, protein and whole food help hold the line.
grade: A
safety: none
when: plateau | onboarding | myth
aud: all
from: SCI-05-001, YT-03-011, CGF-05-002, CGF-05-001, CGF-03-001

### FC-140
title: Breakfast isn't essential
claim: Skipping breakfast doesn't make you eat more overall: in trials, breakfast eaters ate ~260 kcal more a day and weren't lighter.
why: The next meal grows only slightly, so total intake falls. The pre-dawn hormone surge has already fueled you.
grade: A
safety: none
when: myth | plan
aud: all
from: YT-05-003

### FC-141
title: Smart scales misread fasting
claim: Body-fat scales and scans often report "muscle loss" during fasting that is really water and glycogen, and it returns when you eat.
why: These devices estimate lean mass assuming steady body water; fasting sheds water, which they read as lost muscle.
do: Track strength and waist size instead during fasting periods.
grade: B
safety: none
when: myth | long
aud: all
from: YT-02-006, CGF-00-016

### FC-143
title: That "91% heart risk" headline
claim: The 2024 "8-hour eating raises heart-death risk 91%" story came from an unreviewed conference abstract built on two days of diet recall.
why: Participants weren't intentionally fasting, and diet quality and baseline differences weren't reported, which the AHA's own reviewer flagged.
grade: B
safety: none
when: myth
aud: all
from: YT-07-007

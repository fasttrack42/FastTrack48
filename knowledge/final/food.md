# Final cards: Food & drinks: what to drink while fasting, how to break a fast, what to eat between fasts.

### FC-055
title: Use fasting aids sparingly
claim: A spoon of olive oil or chia soaked in water can rescue a fast. Use one only when it decides whether you finish.
why: Tiny amounts of fat or fiber barely raise insulin and blunt cravings; taken out of habit, they replace energy your fat stores would otherwise supply.
do: Ask: "Will this help me fast another 12 hours?"
grade: D
safety: none
when: hunger | long | fluids
aud: all
from: YT-07-004, CGF-11-011, YT-07-005, CGF-15-012

### FC-056
title: Sweeteners make it harder
claim: Diet drinks, sweeteners and "naturally flavored" drinks tend to make fasting harder; swapping diet drinks for water improved weight loss in a trial.
why: Sweet taste may prime appetite and insulin without delivering energy, so cravings stay alive, especially near the end of a fast.
do: Fast with plain drinks. If you need sweetness, use a little and drop it if hunger worsens.
grade: B
counter: Whether sweeteners raise insulin in humans is inconsistent across studies.
safety: none
when: fluids | hunger | break
aud: all
from: CGF-09-006, CGF-00-007, YT-02-004, YT-08-001

### FC-060
title: Yes while fasting
claim: Water (still or sparkling), any plain or herbal tea, lemon, cinnamon, salt, and plain vitamins or electrolytes.
why: These carry almost no energy, so insulin stays low, while they ease hunger, hydrate, and keep your daily rituals.
grade: D
safety: none
when: fluids | start
aud: all
from: CGF-11-001, CGF-04-001, CGF-01-021, CGF-16-001, YT-01-010, CGF-09-008

### FC-061
title: No while fasting
claim: Sugar, honey, juice, sweetened or flavored drinks, and sweeteners break or undermine a fast.
why: Sugars raise insulin outright. Juice concentrates sugar without fiber. Sweeteners and flavor additives tend to stoke appetite.
grade: D
safety: none
when: fluids | myth
aud: all
from: CGF-11-002, CGF-11-010

### FC-064
title: Drink on a schedule
claim: Aim for about 2 liters of fluid on fasting days. Start the morning with a glass of water.
why: Much of your usual water comes with food; as insulin falls you also shed retained water and salt. It's easy to forget to drink.
do: A reminder every couple of hours helps early on: water with a pinch of salt, or tea.
grade: D
safety: none
when: fluids | start | stage:12-20
aud: all, beginner
from: CGF-11-005, YT-05-009

### FC-067
title: Teas that help
claim: Green tea, cinnamon or ginger teas and bitter herbal teas may take the edge off hunger; peppermint eases bloating.
why: Warm, flavorful, zero-energy drinks fill the stomach and keep the ritual. Green-tea catechins and cinnamon have mild appetite effects in small studies.
grade: C
safety: none
when: fluids | hunger | symptom:bloating
aud: all
from: CGF-11-007, CGF-16-003, CGF-15-010, YT-02-017, YT-04-006

### FC-070
title: Break it gently
claim: After fasts over 24 hours, start with a small "pre-meal": cooked vegetables or avocado. Main meal 30 minutes later.
why: Digestion slows during a fast. A small, easy first portion wakes it gently and lets the urge to feast pass.
do: Under 24 hours, no special care is needed. For longer fasts, prepare the small dish in advance.
grade: D
safety: none
when: break
aud: all
from: YT-02-001, CGF-15-004, CGF-00-009, CGF-15-005

### FC-071
title: Go easy on these first
claim: Right after a fast, nuts, seeds, nut butters, raw or fibrous vegetables, eggs, dairy and wine are the likeliest to upset your stomach.
why: They demand more stomach acid and enzymes, which take time to return after a fast. Tolerance varies by person.
grade: D
safety: none
when: break | symptom:bloating
aud: all
from: YT-02-002, CGF-15-006

### FC-072
title: Eat normally, not a feast
claim: The most common fasting mistake is eating too much afterward. Eat a normal-sized meal, as if the fast hadn't happened.
why: The fast works because stored fat covers the skipped meals. After a 36-hour fast people eat only ~20% more, but cramming a day's food into one meal cancels the gain.
do: Serve a normal portion, eat slowly, drink water, and wait before seconds. No extra calorie-cutting on top.
grade: B
safety: none
when: break | eat
aud: all
from: YT-01-013, CGF-13-006, CGF-03-010, CGF-15-007, YT-02-003, CGF-15-008

### FC-073
title: Carbs last
claim: Eat protein and vegetables first and carbs last. The same meal raised blood sugar about 40% less that way.
why: Protein, fat and fiber eaten first slow stomach emptying and prime gut hormones, so starch arrives later and gentler.
do: Leave bread, rice or potato for the end of the meal. Ask restaurants to bring bread with the main course.
grade: B
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all, t2d
from: YT-03-005

### FC-074
title: No naked carbs
claim: Pair starch with acid, protein or fat: vinegar, lemon, pickles, or cooked-then-cooled rice and potatoes blunt the blood-sugar spike.
why: Acid slows starch digestion and stomach emptying; cooling cooked starch creates resistant starch; less processed versions (steel-cut vs instant oats) absorb slower.
grade: B
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all, t2d
from: YT-03-007, CGF-11-008, YT-06-007, YT-06-008

### FC-075
title: Fullness hormones from food
claim: Protein, natural fat and fiber trigger your gut's own fullness hormones, the same pathway weight-loss drugs amplify.
why: These nutrients slow stomach emptying and release GLP-1; fiber feeds gut bacteria whose fatty acids boost it further.
do: Build meals around protein, natural fats and fibrous vegetables to stay full longer.
grade: B
counter: Natural GLP-1 rises are far smaller than drug effects.
safety: none
when: eat | break | hunger
aud: all
from: YT-04-012

### FC-076
title: Real food beats processed
claim: Ultra-processed food made people eat about 500 kcal more per day than whole food with the same nutrients.
why: Processing strips fiber and structure, speeds eating and absorption, and weakens fullness signals; sugar and refined flour do this most.
do: Fill eating windows with recognizable whole foods; cut sugar and white flour first.
grade: A
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all
from: CGF-01-016, CGF-01-017

### FC-077
title: Breakfast food is often dessert
claim: Cereals, muffins, pastries, sweetened yogurt and breakfast bars are often dessert in disguise, and leave you ravenous by mid-morning.
why: Refined carbs spike glucose and insulin, which then overshoot; an instant-oatmeal breakfast led children to eat more later than an omelet did.
do: If you eat breakfast, make it eggs, plain yogurt or whole oats, or simply delay your first meal.
grade: B
safety: none
when: eat | break
aud: all
from: YT-03-013

### FC-078
title: Sugar and liver fat
claim: Sugar's fructose is turned into fat in the liver. Cutting added sugar shrank liver fat by about a third in eight weeks.
why: Fructose is processed almost only by the liver. Fatty liver affects a quarter or more of adults, usually silently; 5:2 fasting and low-carb both halved it in a trial.
grade: A
safety: none
when: eat | onboarding
aud: all, t2d
from: YT-03-014, YT-04-001, YT-04-002, YT-04-003

### FC-079
title: Eat earlier when you can
claim: The same meal raises blood sugar and insulin more in the evening. A lighter, earlier dinner suits your body clock.
why: Insulin sensitivity is lowest at night; eating at biological night (shift work, late meals) worsens glucose and lowers fullness hormones.
do: Make dinner lighter and earlier; on night shifts, eat the main meal before the shift.
grade: A
counter: In a tightly controlled trial, big breakfast vs big dinner didn't change weight, only hunger.
safety: none
when: eat | plan
aud: all
from: CGF-12-006, CGF-12-007, CGF-12-008, YT-05-007

### FC-080
title: Protein and lifting protect muscle
claim: Strength training and enough protein when you eat are what protect muscle during fasting; fasting alone doesn't.
why: Muscle responds to load. Fasting combined with resistance training preserves lean mass far better than fasting alone.
do: Lift or do bodyweight training 2–3 times a week; aim for ~1.2–1.6 g protein per kg body weight on eating days.
grade: A
safety: none
when: eat | between | myth
aud: all
from: CGF-03-005, CGF-03-012

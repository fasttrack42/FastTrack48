# Final cards: Mind & habits: hunger, cravings, cues, motivation, sleep and stress (psychology and neuroscience).

### FC-023
title: A rhythm, not a punishment
claim: Fasting isn't penance for overeating. Use it as a planned rhythm, not to "make up" for a meal.
why: Restricting to compensate is a recognized warning pattern for disordered eating, and it turns food into guilt.
grade: D
safety: caution
when: between | break
aud: all
from: CGF-10-003

### FC-050
title: Hunger comes in waves
claim: Hunger builds, peaks and fades, often within 30–45 minutes, even if you eat nothing. It doesn't keep climbing.
why: Ghrelin spikes around your usual mealtimes and then drops on its own, whether or not food arrives.
do: Drink something warm, start a task, step outside. Plan a walk or call for your usual mealtime.
grade: B
safety: none
when: hunger | stage:12-20 | stage:20-24 | stage:2-5
aud: all
from: CGF-09-001, SCI-91-004, YT-02-015, CGF-06-011

### FC-051
title: Mornings are easiest
claim: Most people are least hungry around 8 a.m. and hungriest around 8 p.m., regardless of when they last ate.
why: Appetite follows the body clock, and pre-dawn hormones release stored fuel before you wake.
do: Extending the overnight fast by delaying breakfast is often the easiest first step.
grade: B
safety: none
when: plan | hunger | start
aud: beginner, all
from: CGF-09-002, YT-03-015

### FC-052
title: Much hunger is learned
claim: Clocks, places, screens and smells can trigger hunger like Pavlov's bell. Notice the cue and name it: cue, not need.
why: Repeatedly pairing food with times and situations conditions the brain to release saliva, insulin and hunger on cue.
do: Eat only at a table; don't cook, shop or work in the kitchen on fasting days.
grade: A
safety: none
when: hunger | between | plan
aud: all
from: CGF-09-003, CGF-09-004, CGF-09-007, YT-02-014

### FC-053
title: An if-then plan
claim: Decide in advance what you'll do when hunger hits: "If I open the fridge, I walk for ten minutes."
why: Pre-planned responses to specific cues reliably beat willpower. Swapping a habit (tea instead of the evening snack) works better than dropping it.
do: Keep 3–5 quick actions ready: water, tea, a walk, a call, a small chore.
grade: A
safety: none
when: hunger | plan
aud: all
from: CGF-09-009, CGF-09-005

### FC-054
title: Make hunger your friend
claim: Hunger during a fast means you're on track, not in danger. With regular fasting, appetite tends to fall over time.
why: Hunger is mostly a passing hormonal and habit signal; treating it as an expected companion lowers anxiety, and the body adapts with practice.

### FC-097
title: Give it a month
claim: The first few fasts are usually the hardest. Judge fasting by your fifth attempt, not your first.
why: Hunger habits, conditioned responses and fuel switching all adapt with repetition; your body learns to move into fat-burning faster.
do: The night before, eat a simple home-cooked meal low in sugar and starch; it makes the next day easier.
grade: D
safety: none
when: start | between | plan
aud: beginner
from: CGF-12-002, YT-05-008

### FC-098
title: Ending early still counts
claim: A fast that ends early still counts. Stopping at 22 of 24 hours is 22 hours of benefit, not a failure.
why: Benefits build hour by hour. Treating partial fasts as wins builds the confidence that makes longer ones possible.

### FC-099
title: A nibble doesn't reset it
claim: A few nuts, a piece of cheese or a bite of salad doesn't reset your fast to zero. Just carry on.
why: A tiny intake bumps insulin briefly, then it falls again and fat-burning resumes. Missing a fast day doesn't undo weeks of routine either.
do: Note it if you like and continue. Back on schedule tomorrow.
grade: D
safety: none
when: hunger | break | between
aud: all
from: YT-01-009, CGF-12-005

### FC-101
title: Not during stress or travel
claim: Long fasts often fail during travel, stress or celebrations. Schedule them for quieter weeks.
why: Fasting is itself a stressor. On top of other stress it raises cortisol and blood sugar and turns from beneficial to draining.
do: In a high-stress period, shorten fasts rather than push harder.
grade: D
safety: none
when: plan | long | plateau
aud: all
from: CGF-00-014, YT-06-010

### FC-102
title: Don't do it alone
claim: People who fast with a partner, friend or group do better, especially at keeping weight off.
why: Shared schedules remove food cues at home, and accountability keeps habits going after the early motivation fades.
do: Invite someone to fast with you, or tell a few supportive people. Not everyone.
grade: B
safety: none
when: plan | between
aud: all
from: CGF-10-010, YT-07-014, YT-02-013, CGF-15-002

### FC-103
title: Food between fasts matters
claim: You can't out-fast an unhealthy diet. If a longer fast leads to overeating, dial back down: not ready yet, not weak.
why: Poor eating between fasts gradually cancels their benefit and makes the next fast harder. Progress sticks when each level is steady before the next.
do: Notice why you eat (habit, comfort, company) without shame, and lengthen fasts only once meals are steady.
grade: D
safety: none
when: between | plan | break
aud: all
from: YT-08-010, YT-08-011, YT-08-012, YT-07-012, CGF-10-011

### FC-105
title: Sleep is part of it
claim: Short sleepers who slept about an hour more ate ~270 kcal less a day and lost weight without any diet advice.
why: Sleep loss raises hunger and food reward and lowers insulin sensitivity. Chronic stress adds cortisol and stress eating.
do: Under 6.5 hours of sleep? An earlier bedtime may be your easiest lever.
grade: A
safety: none
when: plateau | between | symptom:insomnia
aud: all
from: YT-05-006, YT-03-009, YT-03-008, SCI-92-010

### FC-180
title: One slip isn't a lost day
claim: A slip is one meal, not a ruined day. All-or-nothing thinking turns small lapses into big ones; kindness to yourself prevents it.
why: After breaking a rule, restrained eaters often eat far more (the "what-the-hell" effect). Self-compassion after a lapse reduced later overeating; self-criticism increased it.
do: Resume at the next planned point, and talk to yourself as you would to a friend.
grade: B
safety: none
when: break | between | hunger
aud: all
from: SCI-92-001, SCI-92-002

### FC-181
title: Surf the urge
claim: A craving is a wave. Notice where you feel it, breathe, and watch it crest and fade without acting on it.
why: Observing an urge without judging it breaks the automatic craving-to-action chain; brief "urge surfing" training weakened that link in a controlled trial.
grade: B
counter: Tested for smoking; food-specific evidence is smaller, though the mechanism is shared.
safety: none
when: hunger | stage:12-20 | stage:20-24
aud: all
from: SCI-92-003

### FC-182
title: Picture two hours later
claim: Before an unplanned bite, picture how you'll feel in two hours and tomorrow. It measurably cools the craving.
why: Long-term framing recruits the brain's prefrontal control regions and dampens reward-region responses to the craved food.
grade: B
safety: none
when: hunger | eat
aud: all
from: SCI-92-004, YT-05-012

### FC-183
title: Cues are a real force
claim: Food sights, smells and ads reliably drive eating and weight gain, and the urge they create often exceeds the pleasure the food delivers.
why: Dopamine fuels "wanting"; enjoyment runs on separate circuits. Cues inflate wanting without adding liking, so avoiding them is strategy, not weakness.
grade: A
safety: none
when: hunger | plan
aud: all
from: SCI-92-005, SCI-92-006

### FC-184
title: We eat more together
claim: People eat substantially more with others than alone, most of all with friends and family.
why: Shared meals last longer and set portion norms; a meta-analysis found this social facilitation reliable.
do: Plan fasts around social meals rather than fighting them, and decide your portion before you sit down.
grade: A
safety: none

aud: all

### FC-185
title: Next milestone, not the finish
claim: Aim for the next milestone on the dial, not the end of the fast. Motivation rises as a goal gets close.
why: The goal-gradient effect: effort increases with perceived nearness, so a chain of near targets carries you further than one distant finish line.
grade: B
safety: none

### FC-186
title: Bored, or hungry?
claim: Much "hunger" is boredom. People eat to escape the empty feeling of under-stimulation.
why: Eating offers quick stimulation and pulls attention away from an unpleasant state of low engagement.
do: Ask "hungry, or under-stimulated?" A task, a walk or a conversation often answers it.
grade: B
safety: none
when: hunger
aud: all
from: SCI-92-011

### FC-187
title: Finishing builds confidence
claim: Every fast you complete makes the next one feel more doable. Personal success is the strongest source of self-confidence.
why: Self-efficacy grows mainly from mastery experiences, not persuasion, and it predicts whether a new habit lasts.
do: Start with fasts you can finish, then stretch. Count your completed fasts, not your misses.
grade: B
safety: none
when: break | between | start
aud: all

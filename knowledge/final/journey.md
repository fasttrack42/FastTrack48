# Final cards: The fasting journey: what your body is doing, stage by stage (dial milestones and 'why fasting works').

### FC-030
title: After a meal: storing
claim: After eating, blood sugar peaks within about an hour and insulin rises. While insulin is high, fat burning is paused.
why: Insulin moves glucose into cells, banks the surplus as glycogen and fat, and blocks fat release until levels fall.
grade: A
safety: none
when: stage:0-2
aud: all
from: SCI-91-001, CGF-01-005

### FC-031
title: A short walk after eating
claim: A 10–15 minute easy walk after a meal noticeably lowers the blood-sugar and insulin spike from it.
why: Working muscles take up glucose with little need for insulin; light walking beat standing in a meta-analysis of crossover trials.
do: Walk after your biggest meal, especially one with bread, rice, pasta or dessert.
grade: B
safety: none
when: stage:0-2 | eat | break
aud: all, t2d
from: SCI-91-002

### FC-032
title: The 3-hour slump
claim: Ravenous a few hours after a sugary or starchy meal? That's often a blood-sugar rebound, not a real lack of fuel.
why: A fast glucose spike calls up a big insulin surge that can overshoot, dipping glucose below baseline about three hours later.
do: Ride it out with water or tea. Next time, eat protein and vegetables first and carbs last.
grade: B
safety: none
when: stage:2-5 | hunger
aud: all
from: SCI-91-003, YT-03-006

### FC-033
title: Insulin settles, fat unlocks
claim: A few hours after a meal (roughly 3–5, longer with insulin resistance), insulin returns to baseline and fat cells start releasing fat.
why: Low insulin lifts the brake on fat release while the liver draws on glycogen. A snack raises insulin again and pushes this switch back.
do: Let this window happen: no snacks between meals.
grade: B
safety: none
when: stage:5-8 | between
aud: all
from: SCI-91-005

### FC-034
title: The liver takes over
claim: Overnight and into the morning, your liver keeps blood sugar steady, first from stored glycogen, then increasingly by making new glucose.
why: The liver holds about 100 g of glycogen. By around 22 hours, newly made glucose (from glycerol, lactate and amino acids) supplies most of the output.
grade: A
safety: none
when: stage:8-12 | stage:12-20
aud: all
from: CGF-01-006

### FC-035
title: Fridge and freezer
claim: Glycogen is the fridge: small and quick. Body fat is the basement freezer: vast. Low insulin opens the freezer door.
why: The body draws on glycogen first. High insulin keeps fat locked away; as insulin falls and glycogen runs low, stored fat becomes the main fuel.
do: Every hour after the fridge empties is an hour drawn from the freezer. That's what fasting buys.
grade: B
counter: Insulin's block on fat release is textbook. That insulin, rather than overall energy balance, causes obesity is contested.
safety: none
when: stage:12-20 | onboarding
aud: all
from: CGF-05-003, CGF-05-004

### FC-036
title: The switch is personal
claim: The shift to burning mostly fat and ketones typically happens 12–36 hours after your last meal, earlier for some, later for others.
why: Timing depends on how full your glycogen was. A carb-heavy dinner and sitting still push it later; lower-carb meals and movement bring it sooner.
do: A walk during the fast, and a lower-carb last meal, both speed the switch.
grade: B
safety: none
when: stage:12-20 | stage:20-24 | start
aud: all
from: SCI-14-001, YT-01-001, YT-06-012

### FC-037
title: Ketones feed the brain
claim: Your brain isn't glucose-only. As fasting continues it runs increasingly on ketones, and studies find no loss of attention or memory.
why: Liver-made ketones cross into the brain and replace much of its glucose need. That spares muscle protein and keeps you alert.
do: Work and drive normally. If you feel foggy, check salt and water first.
grade: A
counter: The brain's ketone share reaches ~60–70% only after weeks; in the first days it's lower.
safety: none
when: stage:20-24 | stage:24-48 | myth
aud: all
from: CGF-03-008, CGF-01-007, CGF-07-001, CGF-03-009

### FC-038
title: Autophagy, honestly
claim: Fasting boosts autophagy, your cells' recycling of worn-out parts. But no one knows exactly when it starts or peaks in humans.
why: Eating, especially protein and carbs, switches autophagy off through mTOR. Human markers rise after 24–36-hour fasts, but popular hour thresholds come from animal studies.
do: Long gaps without snacks give cells time with the brake released. Treat any hour count as an estimate.
grade: C
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | stage:20-24
aud: all
from: CGF-07-006, CGF-07-007, CGF-07-008, YT-07-003

### FC-039
title: Day two is the hardest
claim: In longer fasts hunger usually peaks around day two, then fades. Many people feel unusually clear and well afterward.
why: Ghrelin, the hunger hormone, peaks on day two and then falls as ketones rise; hospital fasting reports note little hunger after that.
do: If day two is rough, expect it to ease. Don't judge the whole fast by its hardest day.
grade: B
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | long | hunger
aud: all
from: CGF-00-010, CGF-09-010, YT-01-002

### FC-040
title: What the scale is saying
claim: Real fat loss while fasting is about 0.25 kg (½ lb) a day. The rest of the drop is water that returns when you eat.
why: A day's energy (~2,000 kcal) burns about a quarter-kilo of fat. Glycogen is stored with roughly three times its weight in water, which is released, then refilled.
do: Judge a fast by the weight still off a week later.
grade: A
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | long | break | plateau
aud: all
from: CGF-05-013, CGF-00-015, CGF-14-003

### FC-041
title: Growth hormone rises
claim: Fasting is a strong natural booster of growth hormone: about fivefold over a two-day fast.
why: Meals and insulin suppress GH. Without food its pulses grow, helping release fat and restraining muscle-protein breakdown.
grade: B
counter: A fuel-sparing signal, not anti-aging or muscle-building. IGF-1 falls at the same time; studies are small, in lean men.
safety: none
when: stage:48-54
aud: all
from: CGF-01-013, CGF-03-006, CGF-01-014

### FC-042
title: No starvation mode in short fasts
claim: Short fasts don't put you in "starvation mode". Resting energy use rose about 12% by day four.
why: Noradrenaline rises to release stored fuel and keep you alert enough to find food: the opposite of shutting down.
do: Expect energy, not sluggishness. Fatigue usually signals low salt.
grade: B
counter: Small study of lean adults. Much longer fasts do eventually lower energy use.
safety: none
when: stage:48-54 | stage:54-72 | myth
aud: all
from: CGF-01-012, CGF-03-002

### FC-043
title: Saving sugar for the brain
claim: Two to three days in, muscles temporarily resist insulin to save glucose for the brain. It's normal and reverses after eating.
why: Fat released from stores floods muscle and blocks its glucose uptake. Higher readings late in a long fast, or just after it, are expected.
do: Judge insulin health between fasts: repeated fasting with fat loss improves it over weeks.
grade: B
safety: none
when: stage:54-72 | stage:48-54 | break
aud: all, t2d
from: SCI-90-001, CGF-00-003

### FC-044
title: Muscle is spared, not untouched
claim: Protein breakdown is highest in the first days of a fast, then falls sharply as ketones take over. It slows; it never stops.
why: Early glucose comes partly from amino acids. As ketones rise, nitrogen loss drops; in a 10-day trial, strength held while lean tissue declined slightly.
do: Protect muscle with strength training and enough protein on eating days.
grade: A
safety: none
when: stage:72+ | stage:24-48 | myth
aud: all
from: CGF-01-008, YT-02-007, CGF-03-005

### FC-045
title: Renewal: what's known
claim: Multi-day fasts in mice clear old immune cells and trigger regeneration on refeeding. In people this remains unproven.
why: Prolonged fasting lowers IGF-1 and growth signaling, pushing stem cells toward self-renewal; ordinary daily fasting barely moves IGF-1.
grade: C
safety: none
when: stage:72+
aud: all
from: CGF-07-011, YT-05-004, YT-05-005

### FC-046
title: Keep moving
claim: Do your usual activities while fasting: walk, work, lift. Movement protects muscle and bone during longer fasts.
why: Stored fat supplies the energy. Disuse, not fasting itself, drove most muscle and bone loss in famous long fasts done lying still.
do: Walk daily and keep light strength work going; scale back if you feel faint.
grade: B
safety: none
when: stage:72+ | long | stage:24-48
aud: all
from: CGF-14-010, YT-02-012

### FC-108
title: What fasting actually does
claim: Regular fasting lowers weight, insulin, blood sugar, blood pressure and inflammation, and it's simple, free and works with any diet.
why: It restricts all foods some of the time rather than some foods all the time. In trials most benefits track the weight lost, about matching daily dieting at equal loss.
do: Its real edge is adherence: pick the version you can keep.
grade: A
safety: none
when: onboarding
aud: all
from: CGF-04-008, CGF-05-008

### FC-131
title: Prediabetes is common and silent
claim: More than half of US adults have diabetes or prediabetes, and most with prediabetes don't know it.
why: Prediabetes rarely causes symptoms, so it goes unnoticed for years while damage starts.
do: Ask for an HbA1c or fasting glucose test, especially if you carry weight around the waist.
grade: A
safety: none
when: onboarding
aud: all
from: CGF-06-001

### FC-142
title: Fuel for a month
claim: Even a lean adult carries enough body fat to cover more than a month of energy needs.
why: At 70 kg and 15% body fat that's about 10 kg of fat, roughly 80,000 kcal, versus about 2,000 kcal of glycogen.
grade: A
safety: none
when: stage:24-48 | myth
aud: all
from: CGF-03-013

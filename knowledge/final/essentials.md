### FC-001
title: Who shouldn't fast
claim: Don't fast for health if you're under 18, pregnant, breastfeeding, underweight (BMI under about 20), or have ever had an eating disorder.
why: Growth, pregnancy and milk need a steady nutrient supply, low body weight leaves no reserve, and deliberate restriction can reignite disordered eating.
do: If any of these apply, skip fasting and talk to your doctor about what's right for you.
grade: A
safety: stop
when: onboarding | plan
aud: all, women
gate: onboarding

### FC-015
title: Over 65? Protect your muscle
claim: After 65, weight loss takes muscle and bone with it unless you protect them. Choose shorter fasts and eat protein at every meal.
why: Older muscle responds less to protein. In dieting older adults, adding resistance plus aerobic exercise preserved lean mass and bone.
do: Strength-train 2–3 times a week, and stand up slowly to avoid dizziness.
grade: A
safety: caution
when: onboarding | plan | between
aud: all

### FC-016
title: Hungry is fine. Sick is not.
claim: Hunger is expected. Persistent nausea, vomiting, dizziness, exhaustion, or feeling truly unwell mean stop.
why: Hunger comes in waves and passes. These signs fall outside normal adaptation and can mean dehydration, electrolyte trouble or a medication problem.
do: End the fast, eat something gentle, and contact your doctor if it doesn't settle. Stopping isn't failing.
grade: A
safety: stop
when: start | stage:24-48 | stage:72+ | long | symptom:fatigue
aud: all
from: CGF-13-001, CGF-00-013, CGF-15-016, CGF-15-015

### FC-017
title: Beyond 3 days, involve a doctor
claim: Don't fast longer than about three days on your own. Longer fasts need medical supervision, blood tests and a refeeding plan.
why: Very long fasts in 1960s hospital programs caused rare deaths and heart damage. Normal blood tests can hide depleted stores; magnesium ran low in the 382-day fast.
do: For supervised long fasts: salt in your water, a multivitamin, and don't attempt them more than monthly.
grade: B
safety: stop
when: long | plan | stage:72+
aud: all
from: SCI-02-001, CGF-16-009, CGF-01-010, CGF-11-009

### FC-018
title: Refeeding after long fasts
claim: After fasts of five days or more, restart eating with small meals. Refeeding syndrome is rare but dangerous.
why: Food raises insulin, which pulls phosphorus, potassium, magnesium and thiamine into cells; blood levels can crash. Highest risk: BMI under 18.5, recent big weight loss, alcohol misuse, diuretics or antacids.
do: Seek care for weakness, palpitations, confusion or breathlessness. Mild ankle swelling in the first days usually passes.
grade: A
safety: stop
when: break | long | stage:72+
aud: all
from: CGF-14-007, CGF-14-008, YT-02-005, CGF-14-011

### FC-019
title: No alcohol while fasting
claim: Don't drink alcohol during a fast. It stops the liver making glucose, so blood sugar can fall, and it hits much harder.
why: The liver prioritizes clearing alcohol over gluconeogenesis; an empty stomach raises blood alcohol fast. On any day it disrupts sleep and adds empty energy.
do: If you drink, keep it with a meal on an eating day, with water alongside.
grade: A
safety: stop
when: fluids | start | stage:12-20 | stage:24-48
aud: all, t2d, meds
from: YT-07-009, YT-07-010

### FC-021
title: If your period stops
claim: Your cycle may shift in the first months of fasting. If periods stop for three months or more, stop fasting and see your doctor.
why: Occasional delays follow any change in weight, diet or stress. Missing periods mean energy availability is too low for reproductive health.
grade: A
safety: stop
when: between | long
aud: women
gate: female

### FC-029
title: Condition or daily medicine?
claim: Diagnosed condition or daily medication? Turn on Health conditions in Settings to see only the guidance that applies to you.
why: Medicines for blood sugar, blood pressure, mood, thyroid or inflammation change how fasting affects you. Specific guidance is safer than generic warnings.
do: Talk to your doctor before your first fast longer than a day.
